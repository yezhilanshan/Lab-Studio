Abstract
Full-body motion estimation from sparse VR observa-
tions is an inherently under-constrained problem, with only
three 6-DoF trackers (HMD and controllers) available to
infer a full skeletal pose. To address this ambiguity, we in-
troduce a probabilistic framework that models joint orienta-
tions as distributions on SO(3) using the Matrix Fisher dis-
tribution. Instead of predicting a single deterministic pose,
our network outputs a distribution for each joint, whose
mode and concentration directly quantify prediction uncer-
tainty on the rotation manifold. This enables likelihood-
based training and principled uncertainty propagation. At
the core of our model is a causal Transformer encoder
that fuses sparse observations with motion history. We fur-
ther propose region-wise tokens for the torso, arms, and
legs, obtained via attention pooling over local joint features
and semantic VR anchors. These tokens guide compact,
per-region Fisher regression. To ensure kinematic coher-
ence efficiently, we employ a limb refinement module, where
each child joint’s Fisher parameters are conditioned on its
parent’s distribution and the regional context, propagat-
ing pose and uncertainty hierarchically. Extensive exper-
iments on standard sparse-VR benchmarks show that our
approach achieves state-of-the-art performance, while pro-
viding well-calibrated joint-wise uncertainty.

unique challenge: the system observes only three 6-DoF
rigid bodies, with no direct signals for the torso or lower

AMASS, learn a direct mapping from head and hand sig-

Despite these advances, the core challenge of one-to-
many ambiguity in motion estimation from sparse obser-
vations remains largely unresolved. Existing methods ei-
ther oversimplify the solution space, depend excessively on
learned priors, or impose additional hardware requirements.
Our work aims to enhance the robustness of full-body pose
estimation from sparse head–hand observations alone. We
identify three pivotal limitations in current learning-based
frameworks and propose corresponding improvements:
1) Lack of Intrinsic Uncertainty Quantification. Cur-
rent frameworks predominantly perform deterministic re-
gression, failing to model or propagate the intrinsic uncer-
tainty arising from sparse observations [6, 62]. For weakly
constrained joints like those in the lower body, the sys-
tem should explicitly represent orientation ambiguity rather
than commit to a single, potentially inaccurate, estimate.
We therefore formulate pose estimation as a problem of
probabilistic inference over joint rotations. Specifically, we
adopt the Matrix–Fisher distribution on the SO(3) manifold
to represent the belief state of each joint. This approach
is crucial because SO(3) provides a principled mathemati-
cal framework for modeling 3D rotational uncertainty. Un-
like vector space representations, the concentration param-
eters of the Matrix–Fisher distribution naturally and geo-
metrically encode how strongly each joint’s orientation is
constrained by the observations, directly quantifying the
one-to-many ambiguity. This enables training via maxi-
mum likelihood estimation on the manifold and supports
uncertainty-aware applications such as sampling diverse
plausible poses, as shown in Fig.1.
2) Homogeneous Treatment of Heterogeneous Body
Regions. Modeling the body as a monolithic unit overlooks
the significant heterogeneity in motion statistics and ob-
servability across different regions [59]. Sparse signals im-
pose asymmetrical constraints; for instance, the upper body
is well-observed while the lower body is largely uninstru-
mented. To address this, we introduce a region-based pose
representation, partitioning the body into five kinematic re-
gions (torso, left/right arms, left/right legs), each with a
learnable identifier. Our model uses attention-based pool-
ing to first distill region-specific motion contexts, which are
then integrated into a globally coherent full-body pose.
3) Violation of Kinematic Chain Dependencies. Hu-
man motion is inherently hierarchical, with a child joint’s
state (e.g., the wrist) being strongly dependent on its par-
ent’s state (e.g., the elbow and shoulder) [37]. Many con-
temporary methods predict joints in parallel, violating this
kinematic structure and risking physiologically implausible
outputs. We thus design a hierarchical autoregressive de-
coder that strictly adheres to the kinematic tree. It predicts
joint rotations sequentially, propagating the parent’s pose
and, critically, its calibrated uncertainty to the child. This
ensures kinematic coherence and yields anatomically plau-
sible results.
In summary, our main contributions are as follows:
• We propose FisherPoser, a probabilistic framework that
represents joint orientations as Matrix–Fisher distribu-
tions on SO(3), enabling geometrically consistent, cali-
brated per-joint uncertainty estimation and accurate full-
body pose inference under sparse VR observations, with
likelihood-based training on the rotation manifold.
• We design a hybrid hierarchical decoder that couples
region-wise conditioning with limb-wise recursion: the
body is partitioned into five regions (torso, left/right arms,
left/right legs); limb joints are decoded sequentially by
conditioning each child on its parent’s distribution to
propagate pose and uncertainty, while torso joints follow
a direct path for efficiency—balancing local specificity
with global consistency.
• Comprehensive experiments demonstrate that our method
consistently outperforms state-of-the-art baselines un-
der sparse VR tracking, improving spatial accuracy and
perceptual smoothness. Ablations verify that both the
region-aware conditioning and the limb-recursive mecha-
nism are necessary and complementary.
2. Related Work
2.1. Motion Estimation from Sparse Observations
Wearable human pose estimation has evolved along two
primary hardware configurations: multi-inertial measure-
ment unit (IMU) systems that instrument multiple body seg-
ments [15, 19, 46, 50, 54, 59], and minimal virtual real-
ity (VR) setups that rely solely on a head-mounted display
and two hand-held controllers [3, 6, 10, 17, 18, 62]. Al-
though systems with six or more IMUs generally achieve
higher pose accuracy, HMD-based approaches are increas-
ingly favored for their practical deployment advantages. A
growing body of work demonstrates that even three tracking
points can yield plausible full-body motion. For instance,
AvatarPoser [17] introduced a transformer-based architec-
28414
ture to directly regress the body pose from head and hand
inputs; Zheng et al. [62] proposed a two-stage model to bet-
ter capture kinematic joint dependencies. Several works
have integrated physics simulation into the pipeline, such
as QuestSim [49] and QuestEnvSim [23], which leverage
NVIDIA’s Isaac Gym [30] and reinforcement learning for
motion synthesis. To address the inherent one-to-many am-
biguity in sparse-to-dense pose estimation, generative ap-
proaches—including variational autoencoders, normalizing
flows [2, 8], state space model [60, 61] and more recently,
diffusion models [9, 11, 40]—have been employed to sam-
ple multiple plausible pose hypotheses. Another line of
work enhances robustness by incorporating additional sen-
sors, such as pelvis or foot trackers [6, 35, 36], albeit at the
cost of sacrificing minimal setup simplicity.
We adopt the minimal HMD-plus-controllers configu-
ration and tackle input ambiguity through an uncertainty-
centric formulation: instead of a single pose, we predict a
distribution per joint, delivering calibrated confidence for
under-observed limbs and reducing mode collapse while
preserving real-time feasibility.
2.2. Pose Estimation with Uncertainty Modeling
The sparse nature of observations in 3D human pose estima-
tion introduces significant uncertainty, particularly for un-
observed joints, as identical inputs can correspond to mul-
tiple valid pose hypotheses [11, 59, 62]. This challenge
is especially evident in monocular vision-based pose esti-
mation [5, 14, 24, 26, 52], where depth ambiguity consti-
tutes a major source of uncertainty. To address this, a num-
ber of probabilistic and multi-hypothesis approaches have
been proposed. Wehrbein et al. [48] modeled the poste-
rior distribution of 3D poses using normalizing flows, while
MHFormer [26] employed a multi-hypothesis transformer
to capture diverse pose modes via a one-to-many mapping.
More recently, Shan et al. [38] introduced a diffusion-based
aggregation method that enforces joint-wise consistency
across multiple hypotheses. Beyond modeling hypothesis
diversity, uncertainty in deep learning is commonly catego-
rized into aleatoric (data-dependent) and epistemic (model-
dependent) types [21]. Several works leverage these notions
to improve pose estimation—e.g., through uncertainty-
guided sampling and attention mechanisms [25], robust
negative log-likelihood objectives with epistemic uncer-
tainty guidance [58], and uncertainty-weighted sensor fu-
sion in wearable settings [53].
Recently, modeling probability distributions directly on
the rotation manifold for uncertainty-aware rotation esti-
mation has been gaining attention. [7, 13] adopted the
Bingham distribution over unit quaternions to jointly es-
timate a probability distribution over all axes. Mohlin et
al. [32] leveraged the Matrix-Fisher distribution on SO(3)
for deep rotation regression. Although both the Bingham
and Matrix-Fisher distributions share similarities with the
Gaussian distribution in Euclidean space, the Matrix-Fisher
benefits from a continuous rotation matrix representation
and unconstrained distribution parameters, yielding supe-
rior performance in practice [34]. Similarly, recent stud-
ies [22, 56] have also introduced these distribution estima-
tions on the rotation manifold to human pose estimation,
achieving notable performance gains.
Building on these advances, we model a per-joint Ma-
trix–Fisher field on SO(3) and train by maximum likeli-
hood. Unlike prior rotation-level MF regressors, we intro-
duce region-wise conditioning and limb-hierarchical refine-
ment that propagate pose and uncertainty down the kine-
matic chains, expressly tailored to the head–hand sparsity.

strained by the HMD, arms are partially guided by the con-
trollers, while the lower limbs remain almost entirely un-
observed. Treating the body as a single monolithic target
forces one decoder to reconcile disparate statistics and of-
ten dilutes the few informative signals. To address this, we
introduce a region-wise conditioning mechanism that parti-
tions the body into five semantic regions—Torso, Left/Right
Arm, and Left/Right Leg—enabling specialized feature ex-
traction and uncertainty-aware pose estimation for each.

R = {Torso,L-Arm,R-Arm,L-Leg,R-Leg}, with their joint

while preserving global coherence through the autoregres-

4. Experiment
To rigorously evaluate the efficacy of our proposed method,
we first introduce the datasets, evaluation metrics, and base-
line algorithms. We then compare our approach with state-
of-the-art methods, illustrating its effectiveness and perfor-
mance gains. The contribution of each module is quantita-
tively analyzed through ablation experiments. For further
insight, additional case studies and in-depth discussions are
available in the supplementary material.
Datasets. Our experiments are conducted on the
AMASS dataset [29], a large-scale aggregation of human
motion capture data represented in the SMPL body model.
To ensure rigorous evaluation and facilitate fair comparison
with existing literature, we adopt two established experi-
mental protocols from prior work [4, 6].
Protocol 1 (P1) follows the data partitioning strategy de-
scribed in [11, 16, 17, 62], where the CMU [44], BMLrub
[41], and HDM05 [33] subsets are randomly divided by a
90%/10% ratio for training and testing respectively. Pro-
tocol 2 (P2) employs a larger-scale benchmark configura-
tion outlined in [6, 11], utilizing multiple AMASS subsets
[1, 12, 27, 28, 31, 33, 42–45] for training while reserving
the Transitions [29] and HumanEva [39] for evaluation.
Competing methods. We compare the our motion esti-
mation performance with the state-of-the-art methods, in-
cluding AvatarPoser [17], AGRoL [9], AvatarJLM [62],
SAGE [11], HMDPoser [6], RPM[4].
Following [51, 62], our evaluation is conducted using
the following metrics, the mean per-joint rotation error
(MPJRE, in degrees), the mean pre-joint position error
(MPJPE, mm), the mean per-joint velocity error (MPJVE,
mm/s) and the Jitter metric.

by modeling rotations on SO(3) and separating the mode

with Ours-Fisher in Tab. 2, a variant that differs only by the
Matrix–Fisher output head, further confirms that manifold-
aware uncertainty yields better-calibrated predictions.
5. Conclusion
This paper presented a probabilistic framework for full-
body motion estimation from sparse VR observations. By
predicting Matrix–Fisher distributions per joint on SO(3)
and training via likelihood on the rotation manifold, our
approach makes ambiguity explicit and yields calibrated,
joint-level confidence while remaining efficient. Coupled
with causal temporal encoding of head–hand streams, it
produces kinematically coherent and temporally stable mo-
tion under severe under-observation. Ablation studies cor-
roborate the effectiveness of our design and indicate practi-
cal deployment potential.
Limitation and future works. Auto-regressive architec-
tures may exhibit drift failures in long-term series inference.
Future work includes richer anchors (contacts and terrain
cues); Extending to real-world, long-horizon sessions with
latency and drift, and to interaction scenarios with objects,
will further validate the approach.
