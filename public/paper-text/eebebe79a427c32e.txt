Large Language Model-Driven Channel Prediction
in Cell-Free mMIMO Systems
Baolin Chong , Graduate Student Member, IEEE, Hancheng Lu , Senior Member, IEEE,
Dusit Niyato , Fellow, IEEE, and Arumugam Nallanathan , Fellow, IEEE
Abstract—The channel state information (CSI) acquisition
plays a pivotal role in cell-free (CF) massive multiple-input-multi-
output (mMIMO) systems. However, conventional pilot-based
channel estimation incurs prohibitive overhead costs as user
density and mobility increase. To address this, we propose
a multi-slot alternating estimation–prediction (MAEP) frame-
work, which leverages temporal correlation to predict future
CSI directly and thereby drastically reduce pilot overhead.
The efficacy of the proposed framework hinges on prediction
accuracy. Inspired by the remarkable modeling capabilities of
large language models (LLMs) and their demonstrated effi-
cacy in cross-modal applications, we introduce an LLM-driven
channel predictor termed frequency-temporal alignment with
LLM (FTAlign-LLM). FTAlign-LLM bridges the modality gap
between CSI and the LLM’s feature space through three key
components: 1) a multi-scale CSI attention (MSCA) network
for extracting rich spatiotemporal features across frequency and
delay domains, 2) a frequency–temporal feature fusion (FTFF)
network that fuses these features and aligns them with the
LLM’s feature space, and 3) the utilization of parameter-efficient
fine-tuning for LLM adaptation. Extensive results demonstrate
that FTAlign-LLM significantly outperforms benchmarks in pre-
diction accuracy. Concurrently, the MAEP framework achieves
substantial improvements in sum spectral efficiency, particularly
when a large number of access points are deployed in CF
mMIMO systems.
Index Terms—Cell-free massive multiple-input multiple-
output, channel prediction, large language model, fine-tuning.

handover procedures. Cell-free (CF) mMIMO presents a
promising paradigm shift, eliminating cell boundaries to
become a critical enabler for 6G [3], [4], [5]. In CF mMIMO,
a multitude of distributed access points (APs), coordinated by
a central processing unit (CPU), collectively serve users across
an expansive geographical area [6]. This system mitigates ICI
and handover-related inefficiencies, affording more uniform
user performance and harnessing substantial macro-diversity
gains to enhance reliability [7], [8], [9].
Accurate channel state information (CSI) is paramount for
CF mMIMO performance, underpinning essential techniques
like precoding and resource allocation [5], [10]. While mini-
mum mean squared error (MMSE) estimation is ubiquitously
adopted [3], and advanced methods such as deep learning [11],
joint channel estimation/data detection [12], and joint channel
estimation/user message detection [13] have been investigated,
these methods primarily rely on pilot transmissions for CSI
acquisition. However, with rapidly increasing user density,
pilot-based CSI acquisition emerges as a critical bottleneck
impeding system scalability. This stems from a fundamental
trade-off: assigning shared, non-orthogonal pilots results in
severe pilot contamination, degrading estimation accuracy,
whereas allocating unique orthogonal pilots incurs prohibitive
overhead, significantly reducing data transmission resources.
Furthermore, high-mobility scenarios, characterized by shorter
channel coherence intervals, exacerbate this issue. Under such
dynamic conditions, pilot overhead becomes more acute [14],
and channel aging effects further degrade system performance
[15]. Consequently, exclusive dependence on pilot transmis-
sions for CSI acquisition has become increasingly insufficient
to satisfy the stringent operational demands and performance
targets of future CF mMIMO systems.
Existing pilot-based channel estimation in CF mMIMO
systems often overlooks the inherent temporal correlation
across coherence slots [16], which arises from the quasi-static
scattering environment over short durations. Leveraging this
temporal dependency, channel prediction has been proposed
as a solution whereby future CSI can be obtained predictively,
potentially circumventing the need for pilot-based estimation
in every interval [17]. Consequently, predictive CSI acquisition
holds significant potential for reducing the overhead associated
with channel estimation and mitigating the detrimental effects
of channel aging, particularly under high-mobility conditions.
Pioneering work in [18] demonstrated that prediction mitigates

Inspired by this finding, [19] proposed a prediction-assisted
CSI acquisition framework for mMIMO, employing a convo-
lutional neural network (CNN) coupled with an autoregressive
predictor to reduce pilot overhead. However, its recursive
nature, where predictions depend on prior predictions, leads
to substantial accuracy degradation due to error accumulation.
To address this, [14] proposed an alternating estimation and
prediction framework for CF mMIMO. It predicts future CSI
using multiple, non-contiguous past estimated CSI instances,
thereby avoiding recursive error propagation and lowering
overhead compared to continuous estimation. Nevertheless,
this framework suffers from drawbacks including lengthy
initialization, significant storage requirements for historical
CSI, and degraded prediction accuracy, especially for high-
mobility users.
To address these challenges, we introduce a multi-slot
alternating estimation-prediction (MAEP) framework for CF
mMIMO systems. Within the MAEP framework, CSI acquisi-
tion alternates between multi-slot estimation (pilot-based) and
multi-slot prediction phases. Specifically, CSI for a block of
consecutive slots is first obtained via pilot-based estimation.
Subsequently, the CSI for the immediately following block of
consecutive slots is predicted based exclusively on the CSI
acquired during the preceding estimation phase. This frame-
work maintains the pilot overhead for CSI acquisition at a
manageable level by interleaving prediction phases. Crucially,
by grounding predictions solely on recently estimated CSI,
MAEP prevents error propagation and eliminates the need
for extensive historical data storage, thus overcoming key
limitations of prior alternating frameworks.
The effectiveness of the proposed MAEP framework for
CF mMIMO systems fundamentally relies on accurate CSI
prediction. Given the rapid advancements and substantial
potential demonstrated by deep learning (DL), it has emerged
as the predominant approach for channel prediction tasks. Con-
sequently, various classical neural network architectures have
been investigated, including multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs),
CNNs, long short-term memory (LSTM), Transformers, etc.
For instance, [14] employed MLPs for channel prediction.
However, this predictor is considered relatively rudimentary
and exhibits limitations in predictive efficacy, notably its
inability to effectively capture spatial channel correlations
arising from factors such as dense AP antenna deployments
and specific characteristics of the scattering environment in
practical systems. In contrast, CNNs have shown promise
by treating CSI prediction akin to image processing [20],
with 3D complex CNNs specifically designed to capture
spatio-temporal relationships in the angle-delay domain [21].
Recurrent architectures like LSTMs have addressed specific
issues such as training acceleration in one-bit mMIMO [22]
and channel aging in satellite communications [23]. More
recently, powerful Transformer models have been explored
for sequential [24] and parallel prediction (to mitigate error
propagation and mobility impacts) [25], leading to specialized
variants like Gruformer [26] and CS3T-UNet [27] for vehicle-
to-everything communications and generic mMIMO systems,
respectively. Despite the significant progress achieved by
these DL-empowered predictors, their predictive capabilities
are often constrained by the scale of the classical neural
networks. Many existing predictors still struggle to precisely
model the complex spatio-temporal-frequency relationships
inherent in wireless channels, frequently exhibiting suboptimal
performance, particularly within challenging high-mobility
scenarios.
Recently, large language models (LLMs), pre-trained on
extensive text corpora, have gained prominence for their
remarkable semantic understanding, reasoning capabilities,
and success in natural language processing [28]. Crucially,
their ability to rapidly adapt to diverse downstream tasks,
such as time-series forecasting [29] and trajectory predic-
tion [30], [31], through fine-tuning with limited task-specific
data presents a compelling paradigm for overcoming limita-
tions in existing channel predictors. Following this trend, an
LLM-driven predictor denoted as LLM4CP was proposed in
[32], adapting a pre-trained LLM for the channel predic-
tion task via fine-tuning. However, the simple preprocessing
adopted in LLM4CP may not suffice to effectively extract
the intricate propagation characteristics embedded within CSI,
given that CSI represents highly complex, high-dimensional
structured data. Furthermore, a fundamental domain gap exists
between the textual data LLMs are primarily designed for
and the numerical, structured nature of CSI data [33]. This
discrepancy can lead to a modality mismatch, stemming
from differing statistical distributions and underlying structural
semantics, potentially hindering optimal performance.
To overcome these issues, we introduce an LLM-derived
predictor specifically tailored for CSI acquisition, termed
frequency-temporal alignment with LLM (FTAlign-LLM). Our
predictor incorporates several key innovations. Specifically, we
employ a multi-scale CSI attention (MSCA) network designed
to comprehensively extract features from CSI across both the
delay and frequency domains by integrating local and global
channel attention mechanisms. Subsequently, we introduce a
frequency–temporal feature fusion (FTFF) network, which is
responsible for effectively fusing the multi-domain features
extracted by the MSCA and projecting them into a feature
space that is semantically aligned with the LLM. Beyond mere
feature fusion, FTAlign-LLM treats cross-domain alignment
as retrieval: a structured (delay) view queries a fine-grained
(frequency) view to retrieve evidential details with low-entropy
attention. This reasoning-style aggregation contrasts with the
mixture-style addition used in LLM4CP [32], which blends the
two views as interchangeable features and dilutes asymmetric
index-to-evidence roles. To facilitate the efficient adaptation
of the LLM to the channel prediction task, we employ a
parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) strategy, wherein only a
small subset of the model’s parameters is retrained. The main
contributions of our work are summarized as follows:
• We propose the MAEP framework for CF mMIMO
systems, where channel estimation and channel predic-
tion are alternately performed across multiple coherence
slots to effectively reduce pilot overhead and conserve
coherence length, and analyze the system performance
under this operational framework.

II. SYSTEM MODEL
In this section, we provide a detailed explanation of the
system model of the CF mMIMO with channel aging. We
commence by presenting the network architecture and the
channel aging model. Subsequently, the procedures for channel
estimation and uplink data transmission related to the system’s
operation are presented.
A. Network Model
We consider an uplink CF mMIMO system consisting of
M APs, each equipped with N antennas, that collaboratively
serve K single-antenna users, as illustrated in Fig. 1. Each AP
is connected to a CPU via the fronthaul link. The system oper-
ates under a time-division duplexing (TDD) protocol, where
the channel remains stationary within each coherence slot but
evolves between successive slots. Furthermore, channel aging
is characterized by the property that the channel in a given
coherence slot exhibits correlation with that in previous slots
according to the auto-correlation function (ACF) [18].
Due to the limited physical space for antenna deployment
and poor scattering environments, the actual propagation chan-
nels typically exhibit spatial correlation. Thus, the channel

III. MULTI-SLOT ALTERNATING ESTIMATION PREDICTION
AND PERFORMANCE ANALYSIS
In this section, we propose a multi-slot alternating
estimation-prediction (MAEP) framework for CF mMIMO
systems with channel aging, leveraging temporal autocorre-
lation, and analyze the performance under this framework.
A. Multi-Slot Alternating Estimation-Prediction Framework
Conventional TDD comprises two types of frame structures,
corresponding respectively to channel estimation and uplink
data transmission, where the CSI obtained during the channel
estimation phase is utilized in uplink data transmission, as
shown in Fig. 2. In each coherence slot, channel estimation
occupies a significant amount of resources, and obtaining CSI
is crucial for data transmission in CF mMIMO systems. As
the number of users increases, the overhead associated with
channel estimation takes an increasingly large portion of each
coherence slot, particularly in mobile scenarios characterized

by short coherence intervals. To mitigate this issue, we con-
sider utilizing the temporal correlation between successive
coherence slots for channel prediction and propose the MAEP
framework, as illustrated in Fig. 2. Specifically, the MAEP
framework operates in a periodic manner, where each period
contains two blocks: an estimation block (EB) spanning O
coherence slots and a prediction block (PB) spanning P coher-
ence slots. During each coherence slot within the EB, each
user transmits pilots; subsequently, each AP estimates the CSI
using MMSE, which is then used for uplink data transmission.
In each coherence slot of the following PB, each AP leverages
the estimated channels from the preceding O coherence slots to
predict the channel based on the temporal correlation between
consecutive coherence slots according to (3). By acquiring CSI
through channel prediction, the significant pilot overhead is
avoided [17], [18], allowing all resources in each coherence
slot to be allocated for data transmission. Therefore, only
uplink data transmission is performed in each coherence slot
in the PB, with the predicted CSI assumed to represent the
true channel.

C. LLM-Derived Backbone Network
LLMs pre-trained on extensive text corpora have demon-
strated exceptional capabilities in learning rich semantic
representations and performing complex reasoning tasks,
underpinned by their core strengths in cross-domain gener-
alization and the modeling of long-range dependencies [28].
Although initially developed for natural language processing,
these models possess intrinsic architectural advantages that
render them broadly applicable to a variety of structured
sequence modeling problems beyond text. Recent advance-
ments have shown that, with suitable fine-tuning, LLMs can be
successfully adapted to non-textual sequence data tasks [29],
[30], [31].
Motivated by these developments, we propose leveraging an
LLM for channel prediction in CF mMIMO systems, aiming
to exploit its potential for capturing the intricate spatiotem-
poral correlations inherent in wireless channels. Despite the
fundamental modality mismatch between textual and CSI data,
manifested in their distinct statistical properties and structural
“semantics”, we contend that LLMs remain highly suitable
for this task for serval reasons. First, the spatiotemporal
structures of wireless channels exhibit complex, long-range
dependencies that are conceptually analogous to syntactic and
semantic dependencies in language sequences. Second, the
strong generalization capacity of LLMs, honed through large-
scale pretraining, enables them to adapt effectively to novel,
structured data domains when appropriately guided. From
a mathematical perspective. CSI prediction is a continuous
sequence to sequence regression problem: given an O-step
history we learn a mapping to a P-step future by minimizing
the expected squared error (the L2 risk). The Bayes-optimal
predictor is the conditional expectation of the future given
the history, and attention based Transformers have sufficient
expressive power to approximate such continuous sequence
mappings on compact domains. Therefore an LLM backbone
is in principle adequate for this task. Next, self attention can
be viewed as a similarity weighted averaging mechanism:
the query induces data dependent, nonnegative weights over
past time and frequency points, and the output aggregates the
corresponding values. This yields natural denoising through
averaging and enables long range coupling that is not restricted

To verify the effectiveness of each individual module, we

For FTAlign-LLM without the LLM, the GPT-2 based LLM
is removed, while all other components are kept intact. It
is evident that removing any of these modules results in
performance degradation, highlighting the indispensable role
each plays in ensuring the effectiveness of FTAlign-LLM
within CF mMIMO systems. Note that Table V reports NMSE
and sum SE averaged over all tested normalized Doppler

VI. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK
In this paper, we have proposed the MAEP framework for
CF mMIMO systems to reduce pilot overhead and enhance
sum SE. To enable accurate channel acquisition within MAEP,
we have further developed an LLM-driven predictor, FTAlign-
LLM. This predictor can bridge the modality gap between
CSI measurements and the LLM’s feature space through
three carefully designed components: 1) an MSCA network
for multi-domain feature extraction, 2) an FTFF network for
feature fusion and alignment, and 3) a PEFT strategy for
lightweight adaptation of the LLM backbone. Extensive results
has demonstrated that FTAlign-LLM outperformed all bench-
marks in NMSE, particularly under high-mobility conditions,
and that the overall MAEP framework can deliver substantial
gains in sum SE, especially when deploying large numbers of
APs and users.
Our investigation of LLM-driven channel prediction in CF
mMIMO remains at a preliminary theoretical stage and war-
rants further research. First, for practical deployment and mod-
eling realism, two aspects merit attention:(i) computational
footprint—leveraging task placement and model-compression
strategies to meet real-time latency constraints in distributed
CF systems; and (ii) temporal-correlation modeling under
heterogeneous mobility. While our dataset generation already
samples non-uniform Doppler across users or links, the ana-
lytical aging model adopts the normalized ACF, which implies
identical temporal correlation across links. In future work,
we will relax this simplification by adopting per-link ACFs,
allowing anisotropic and time-varying correlation functions,
and exploring data-driven correlation learned from measure-
ments or high-fidelity simulators. In addition, two practical
motivations point toward a new line of work. First, the split
between estimation and prediction slots has a direct impact
on both spectral efficiency and robustness, yet there is no
systematic procedure for choosing it in practice. Second,
user mobility is heterogeneous and time varying, suggesting
that prediction frequency should adapt—stationary users can
economize on computation, while highly mobile users need
more frequent updates to maintain reliability. Motivated by
these factors, we leave for future work the conception and
design of an adaptive MAEP framework that dynamically
adjusts the per-user prediction span and estimation cadence
to balance spectral efficiency, robustness, and computational
cost.
