Abstract—Integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) is
expected to enable the fifth-generation (5G) networks to provide
ubiquitous communication and sensing. However, some high-
dynamic scenarios hinder applications of conventional ISAC
schemes owing to the high overhead and poor real-time per-
formance. In this paper, we design a novel ISAC architecture
and propose a large language model (LLM) based two-stage
beamforming prediction scheme. Specifically, in the first stage, we
develop an LLM-based approach to predict the future channel
state information (CSI) according to the history echoes. Via the
data preprocessing and supervised fine-tuning, the LLM can
achieve effective channel prediction task with unstructured data.
In the second stage, according to the predicted/estimated CSI,
we formulate a beamforming optimization problem to maximize
the achievable sum rate while satisfying the quality of service
(QoS). Then, we propose a Primary-dual network with the
unsupervised adversarial learning to handle it, facilitating the
on-line beamforming. Simulation results verify that, compared
with the benchmarks, our proposed beamforming prediction
scheme not only enjoys a higher channel prediction accuracy
but also achieves a better balance between the performance and
computational complexity.
Index Terms—Beamforming prediction, integrated sensing and
communication, joint range and angle estimation, large language
model, multiple signal classification.

I. INTRODUCTION
NOWADAYS, diverse applications in fifth-generation (5G)
networks, such as low-altitude economy (LAE) and
autonomous driving, require ubiquitous communication and
sensing [2]. However, conventional communication or sensing
systems may have difficulty facilitating specific applications
due to their single functionality [3]. Fortunately, integrated
sensing and communication (ISAC) is becoming a promising
technology to enable diverse applications effectively [4]. In
particular, ISAC can integrate the communication and sensing
functionalities into a single platform with shared spectrum and
hardware, enjoying the advantages of low cost and high spec-
trum efficiency [5]. Moreover, ISAC also introduces mutual
benefits between these two functionalities, i.e., communication
can assist the sensing and vice versa [6], [7]. For example,
Dong et al. in [6] proposed a communication-aided sensing
scheme with two waveform design frameworks. Meng et al. in
[7] investigated sensing-assisted communication in vehicular
networks, and a two-stage ISAC protocol was developed to
boost the communication performance benefit from sensing.
Ye et al. in [8] investigated the ISAC-enabled LAE where an
unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) acts as both the communica-
tion user and sensing target served by a base station (BS),
and proposed a deep reinforcement algorithm to deal with
the joint optimization of transmit beamforming at BS and
UAV trajectory. In [9], Zhang et al. proposed a bistatic ISAC
for LAE to boost the coverage range and sensing accuracy.
Different from the above-mentioned two works, Kuang et al. in
[10] investigated a UAV-enabled ISAC scheme, where a UAV
with a moveable-antenna array (MA) provides communication
and sensing services for the LAE applications simultaneously.
In many scenarios, wireless channels can be highly dynamic
due to the high mobility of users or targets. This poses
stricter demands on real-time channel estimation and resource
allocation [11]. Although diverse channel estimation algo-
rithms exist, such as minimum mean squared error (MMSE)
estimation [12] and deep learning-based methods [13], they
rely heavily on pilot transmissions and channel feedback,
which are time-consuming. Additionally, estimating the chan-
nel per interval significantly increases overhead. Fortunately,
the ISAC-BS can first transmit the probing signal and
then estimate the communication channels before information
transmission, without the need for the pilot transmission and
feedback. Additionally, by leveraging temporal correlations

[14] developed a two-stage beamforming prediction scheme
for ISAC-enabled vehicle-to-infrastructure networks. Specif-

the future channels according to the former estimated channels,
and then jointly optimized the active and passive beamforming
vectors. In [15], Yuan et al. investigated predictive beam-

the echoes to estimate and predict the motion parameters of
vehicles and designed the beamforming vector, achieving less
overhead than the feedback-based counterpart. In addition,
Liu et al. in [16] tracked and predicted the movement of each
vehicle according to the extended Kalman filtering, and then
allocated the transmit power to boost the communication and
sensing performance. On the other hand, many deep learn-

proposed to facilitate online deployment [17]. For example,
Song et al. in [18] proposed an unsupervised learning based
beamforming scheme, where the penalty-version of objective
function was utilized as the loss function to train the NN
to learn the mapping from the channel to the corresponding
beamforming vector. In [19], Zhang et al. developed a deep
reinforcement learning based scheme to deal with the joint
waveform and beamforming design in a trial-and-error manner.
Nevertheless, the above-mentioned schemes may struggle in
dealing with the non-convex optimization with complicated
constraints [20].
Recently, large language models (LLMs) have demonstrated
remarkable semantic understanding and reasoning capabilities,
achieving significant success in natural language processing
(NLP) [21], [22]. Several major applications, such as chat
generative pre-trained transformer (ChatGPT) [23] and Sora
[24], have transformed the paradigm of human-computer inter-
action. For LLMs, the principle of digital content generation
is to predict the next token according to the given preceding
context. Moreover, LLMs can capture long-distance indepen-
dence thanks to the attention mechanism. Consequently, LLMs
have demonstrated the powerful prediction capabilities. Addi-
tionally, LLMs can be adapted to various downstream tasks,
such as time series forecasting [25] and trajectory prediction
[26], via fine-tuning with task-specific data, enabling their
applications in diverse prediction tasks. For example, Lan et al.
in [26] leveraged the pre-trained LLM to predict the future
trajectories of cars based on the history trajectories. Due to
its high prediction accuracy and generalization, LLM-based
channel prediction is also becoming a hot topic. In [27],
Liu et al. developed a pre-trained LLM-based scheme for
channel prediction and proposed a modal alignment method.
Leveraging the capability of next-token generation of LLMs,
Fan et al. in [28] developed another LLM-based CSI prediction
scheme with a modality alignment approach. Xu et al. in
[29] investigated LLM-enabled near-field communication for
the LAE, where the LLM was utilized as an optimization
solver to handle the beamforming optimization. Nevertheless,
LLM-enabled ISAC is still in its infancy.
To the best of our knowledge, LLM for sensing-aided
communication has not been well studied, which motivates this
work. In this paper, we first design a novel ISAC architecture
to balance the performance and overhead, and then propose
an LLM-based two-stage beamforming prediction scheme. In
addition, comparisons between this work and related ones
are presented in Table I. The contributions of this paper are
summarized as below.
• This paper investigates the LLM for sensing-aided com-
munication in high-dynamic scenarios. We first propose a
novel hybrid ISAC architecture, adopting the paradigm of
“estimate before predicting”. Then, we develop an LLM-
based two-stage beamforming prediction method.
• In the first stage, we develop an LLM-based channel pre-
diction method by learning the mapping from the history
echoes to future channels. To overcome the difficulty of
modality mismatch, we propose the data pre-processing
and embedding modules.

B. ISAC Architecture
Owing to the high mobility of UAVs, the channels between
the ISAC-BS and UAVs are highly time-varying. Conventional
pilot-based channel estimation approaches usually involve
the pilot transmission and channel feedback, undergoing the
challenges of high overhead and latency [16]. In this paper, we
develop a novel ISAC architecture for the channel estimation
and information transmission as shown in Fig. 2. In particular,
the time period T is equally divided into Q > 1 time slots.
In the i-th time slot (i ≤ q), the ISAC-BS first transmits
the probing signal and then jointly estimates the range and
angle of each UAV via analyzing the echo, bypassing the need
for the channel feedback. Then, the ISAC-BS can conduct
the information transmission based on the estimated CSI. For

III. LLM-BASED CHANNEL PREDICTION
The high-speed movement of UAVs degrades the channel
correlation in a time period and increases the difficulty of
high-accuracy channel prediction, making the conventional
prediction methods intractable. Leveraging the remarkable
capabilities of non-linear fitting and time-series processing,
we develop an LLM-based channel prediction scheme for
the first stage in Fig. 3. In particular, we first design the
data preprocessing and embedding module to overcome the
difficulty of modality mismatch. Then, a generative pre-trained
transformer (GPT)-2 based model with supervised fine-tuning
is developed to fit the non-linear mapping from the history
echoes to the future channels.

C. Backbone Model
LLMs have demonstrated powerful capabilities in dealing
with diverse time series prediction tasks. Although LLMs
were initially used for NLP, they have great potential for
handling various physical-layer tasks in wireless communica-
tions, such as channel prediction and resource allocation. The
main reasons are listed below. First, LLMs excel at solving a
variety of structured modeling problems beyond text. Second,
LLMs demonstrate remarkable generalization and reasoning
capabilities. Pre-trained LLMs can handle unseen data with
satisfactory performance. Third, LLMs can be aligned with
CSI data through data preprocessing and fine-tuning. Inspired
by existing works [27], [28], we use a fine-tuned LLM for
channel prediction.
The GPT-2, which follows the architecture of decoder-only
transformer, is adopted as the backbone model.4
The fine-
tune method is low-rank adaptation (LoRA), which leverages
bypass matrices to approximate the full-parameter fine-tuning
[34]. In particular, we only retrain the Q̃ and Ṽ computations
within the multi-head attention component via the LoRA,
with the remaining components frozen. Consequently, most
parameters of GPT-2 are frozen, and the scale of trainable
parameters is small. This approach not only maintains the
general knowledge within GPT-2 in the pre-training period,
but also reduces the computational complexity significantly.
In addition, this approach can reduce the requirement on
the number of training samples owing to the small scale of
trainable parameters.

VI. CONCLUSION
In this paper, we have developed an LLM-based two-stage
beamforming prediction in the sensing-aided communication
scenario. In the first stage, we have proposed an LLM-
based approach to predict the future CSI according to the
history echoes and the data preprocessing and supervised
fine-tuning methods are developed to align the LLM with
the CSI data. In the second stage, we have formulated a
beamforming optimization problem based on the predicted
CSI and then derived its dual problem. Afterwards, we have
utilized the PD-Net with an unsupervised adversarial learn-
ing manner to fit the mapping from the channels to the
primary and dual variables respectively, facilitating the low-
complexity beamforming. Compared with the benchmarks, the
proposed beamforming prediction scheme not only achieves a
higher channel prediction accuracy for ISAC but also keeps
a better balance between the performance and computational
complexity.
