RaStream: Edge-Deployable Streaming Human
Mesh Recovery from mmWave Radar
Jiazhen Dong and Lei Liu, Senior Member, IEEE
Abstract—Millimeter-wave (mmWave) radar provides a
privacy-preserving sensing modality for human motion analysis,
yet continuous SMPL eXpressive (SMPL-X) recovery on edge
devices remains difficult because accurate reconstruction benefits
from dense volumetric radar observations that preserve weak
articulated-body reflections, while repeatedly processing such
high-dimensional data over time incurs substantial computational
cost. Temporal context is also necessary to resolve frame-
local ambiguity, but the relevant SMPL-X attributes evolve on
different time scales, with body morphology remaining rela-
tively stable and articulated pose, root orientation, and global
translation changing rapidly, which makes a single temporal
update mechanism suboptimal for streaming reconstruction. We
present RaStream, a causal streaming framework that separates
rich volumetric spatial perception from lightweight temporal
reasoning. For each radar observation, a localization-conditioned
spatial encoder first exploits global scene context to estimate a
coarse body location and then focuses detailed reconstruction on
a center-guided region of interest while preserving the native
3D radar structure; the resulting global and local features
are fused into a compact spatial representation, so that high-
dimensional volumetric evidence is extracted only once per ob-
servation and temporal history can subsequently be propagated
through compact representations and fixed-size persistent states.
To further account for the heterogeneous temporal dynamics
of SMPL-X attributes, RaStream maintains a slow morphology
state for shape-related information and a fast motion state for
pose, orientation, and translation. On the 661K-frame M4Human
benchmark, RaStream reduces MVE from 90.90 mm for RT-
Mesh to 84.27 mm with spatial-only inference and further to
72.05 mm with causal temporal refinement, while the Base
configuration requires 26.93 ms per FP32 invocation on a Jetson
Orin Nano. These results demonstrate that continuous radar
mesh recovery can be made practical for edge streaming by ex-
tracting rich volumetric evidence once and propagating temporal
information thereafter in a compact causal form. Our code is
available at https://github.com/LeiLiu-s-Lab/RaStream.git.
Index Terms—mmWave radar, human mesh recovery, dual-
state modeling, streaming inference, edge deployment

cations [1]–[4]. Camera-based pose and mesh recovery has
advanced rapidly [5]–[10], but identifiable visual observations
limit its use in privacy-sensitive environments. Millimeter-
wave (mmWave) radar provides a complementary sensing
modality that is less affected by illumination and partial visual

As illustrated in Fig. 1, our target setting is causal mesh
recovery from a continuous radar stream on a resource-
constrained edge device. Prior RF/mmWave systems have
addressed through-wall sensing [11], [12], skeletal pose es-
timation [13]–[15], fine-grained radar signal generation [16],
gait and identity recognition [17], cross-category action recog-
nition [18], gesture interaction [19], and vital-sign monitor-
ing [20]. Recent work has further extended radar sensing
to real-time continuous activity recognition [21], while radar
benchmarks increasingly support dense human reconstruction
with SMPL [22], [23] and SMPL-X [24], [25]. Unsupervised
learning has also been explored for general radar representa-
tions [26]. These advances demonstrate the growing capability
of radar-based human sensing, but they primarily target signal
generation, recognition, representation learning, or frame-wise
reconstruction. Continuous radar mesh recovery is a different
problem: a practical streaming system must preserve weak
spatial evidence, exploit temporal continuity, and satisfy causal
edge-inference constraints simultaneously.
The central difficulty lies in reconciling spatial fidelity,
temporal context, and streaming efficiency. Point-cloud meth-
ods such as mmMesh [27], MiliPoint [14], mmBody [23],
and P4Transformer [28] reduce computation through peak
extraction or point-level processing. Video-based synthesis can
generate radar training data for privacy-preserving mobile hu-
man sensing [29], but it does not remove the need to preserve
informative evidence in measured radar observations. Peak
selection is computationally attractive, yet it can irreversibly
discard weak responses from limbs, partially occluded body
parts, and other regions that remain informative for dense
articulated reconstruction. Evidence that may be redundant for
coarse localization can still constrain detailed mesh recovery.
Radar-tensor methods such as HuPR [30], RT-Pose [31],
RETR [32], and RT-Mesh [25] preserve denser spatial evi-
dence, but repeatedly processing full radar volumes becomes
expensive in a continuous stream. Related data-generation
work targets rich radar observations for generalized gesture

the same time, frame-wise estimates remain sensitive to body

and clutter, making temporal context important for stable
reconstruction. Existing real-time continuous radar sensing has
primarily focused on activity recognition [21]; short-window
motion models [34] provide only bounded context, whereas
buffering longer radar sequences increases memory use. Re-

with recurrent units derived from LSTM [35], further adds
high-dimensional computation and memory traffic, while bidi-

able during causal inference. The key question is therefore not
how to process more radar data over longer windows, but how
to extract rich spatial evidence once from each observation
and then propagate temporal information in a compact form.
A further requirement arises from the temporal heterogeneity
of SMPL-X attributes. Causal history aggregation can already
stabilize frame-local radar ambiguity, but body shape and
gender remain approximately stable within a subject sequence
and benefit from evidence accumulated over time, whereas
articulated pose, root orientation, and global translation change
rapidly and must remain responsive to the current observation.
A single temporal state therefore faces conflicting update
requirements: rapid updates can make morphology estimates
sensitive to transient radar noise, while aggressive smooth-
ing can suppress genuine motion changes. Separating slowly
varying morphology information from rapidly varying motion
information is therefore expected to provide an additional
refinement beyond generic causal temporal modeling.
These observations suggest three design principles for con-
tinuous mmWave mesh recovery. First, the spatial encoder
should preserve the native volumetric representation while sep-
arating global body localization from subject-centered detailed
reconstruction, so that expensive fine-grained processing is
concentrated where it is most useful. Second, each radar ob-
servation should undergo high-dimensional spatial processing
only once, after which its information should be compressed
into a compact representation for temporal propagation. Third,
temporal reasoning should distinguish slowly varying mor-
phology from rapidly varying motion so that long-term evi-
dence accumulation does not compromise responsiveness to
current observations.
Based on these principles, we propose RaStream, a unified
framework for spatial encoding, causal temporal refinement,
and edge-aware streaming. For each radar observation, a
localization-conditioned spatial encoder preserves the native
3D radar structure and first exploits global scene context to
estimate a coarse body center. Detailed feature extraction is
then focused on a center-guided region of interest, and gated
global–local fusion compresses the resulting evidence into a
compact spatial token. This token serves as the interface be-
tween spatial perception and temporal reasoning, allowing rich
volumetric information to be extracted once from the current
observation and temporal history to be propagated thereafter in
a compact form. Causal history aggregation supplies the main
temporal benefit over spatial-only inference. RaStream further
maintains two persistent temporal states with different roles: a
morphology state accumulates slowly varying evidence about
body shape and related attributes, while a motion state tracks
rapidly changing pose, orientation, and translation, providing
an additional refinement beyond a single recurrent state. In
this way, RaStream avoids both long raw-volume histories and
recurrent processing of high-dimensional radar volumes, while
retaining fixed-size causal temporal memory suitable for edge
deployment.
Table I positions RaStream relative to representative
RF/mmWave sensing and reconstruction methods. Point-cloud
approaches operate on selected reflections, while radar-tensor
or heatmap methods preserve denser spatial evidence but
generally perform frame-wise keypoint estimation, detection,
segmentation, or mesh recovery. Sequence models provide
temporal context, but do not combine native-tensor spatial
encoding with persistent, fixed-state causal refinement for
streaming SMPL-X recovery. RaStream brings these compo-
nents together within a single edge-oriented framework.
The main contributions of this paper are:
• RaStream introduces a localization-conditioned spatial
encoder that preserves the native 3D radar structure,
separates global localization from subject-centered re-
construction, and fuses global and local evidence into a
compact spatial token for temporal reasoning.
• RaStream performs causal temporal reasoning in compact
token space, enabling fixed-memory history aggregation
without future observations, long raw-radar histories, or
recurrent processing of high-dimensional radar volumes;
its slow morphology and fast motion states provide an
additional consistent refinement beyond a comparable
single-state recurrent baseline.
• RaStream is evaluated across spatial-backbone scale,
streaming stride, and temporal unroll horizon, with re-
construction, temporal consistency, latency, memory, and
power profiling on M4Human and Jetson Orin Nano.

II. OVERVIEW
A. Problem Formulation
We formulate streaming radar mesh recovery as a causal,
fixed-memory state estimation problem. At each emitted step,
the model receives only the most recent radar observation and
persistent states propagated from preceding steps, and must
predict the SMPL-X state for the current instant.

B. RaStream Overview
Figure 2 summarizes RaStream. Each current radar win-
dow is processed once by a localization-conditioned spatial
encoder, which preserves the volumetric radar structure while
combining global scene context with subject-centered local
evidence. The encoder compresses this rich observation into a
compact spatial token,
zt = fsp(Xt; θsp). (3)
The token is the interface between costly spatial perception
and lightweight streaming inference: high-dimensional radar
processing is confined to the current window, whereas tempo-
ral history is represented only by persistent compact states.
For single-window evaluation, an auxiliary head produces
ŷsp
t = hsp(zt), (4)
which establishes the body information available from the
current observation alone. The full model refines this estimate
causally through
(ŷt, ut, ht) = ftemp(zt, ut−1, ht−1). (5)
The morphology state ut accumulates slowly varying evidence
for body shape and gender, while the motion state ht tracks
rapidly changing pose, root orientation, and translation. This
division lets RaStream improve temporal stability without
making morphology estimates overly responsive or suppress-
ing genuine motion.
III. RASTREAM SPATIAL ENCODING
A. Spatial Design Principles
Dense radar-based human mesh recovery requires both full-
scene context and spatially concentrated body evidence. The
full radar volume is needed to localize the subject, whereas
fine-grained articulated geometry depends on weak reflections
from limbs and other body parts. Fine-grained multi-user
hand sensing has likewise considered target localization before
detailed pose estimation [38], although its hand-pose setting
differs from full-body SMPL-X recovery. Applying uniform
mesh reasoning to the entire volume spends computation on
background regions, but processing only a local crop requires a
reliable spatial anchor. RaStream therefore decomposes spatial
perception into global body localization followed by subject-
centered local reconstruction.
The spatial representation must also retain weak radar evi-
dence and the geometry of the sensing grid. Peak selection and
point-cloud conversion can remove low-amplitude responses,
while folding one radar axis into the channel dimension weak-
ens the native 3D organization of the tensor. RaStream instead
preserves the three radar axes (H, W, D) throughout spatial
feature extraction. The Tw frames in each short window are
organized only along the input-channel dimension, allowing
3D convolutions to operate on the original radar geometry.

C. Spatial Training Objective
The single-window objective is formulated as a weighted
multi-task loss
Lsp = λβLβ + λposeLpose + λrootLroot
+ λtransLtrans + λgenderLgender + λcenterLcenter. (11)
Here, Lβ, Lpose, Lroot, Ltrans, Lgender, and Lcenter supervise
shape, body pose, root orientation, translation, gender, and
body center, respectively. The scalar coefficients balance the
relative importance of the six tasks; the concrete loss forms
and weights are reported with the implementation details
in Section VI-B. The center term directly supervises the
anchor used for RoI cropping. Localization errors can shift
the crop toward background or exclude weak limb reflections,
degrading mesh and translation estimates; joint supervision
therefore couples localization and reconstruction.
IV. DUAL-STATE CAUSAL TEMPORAL REFINEMENT
A. Temporal Design Principles
Frame-local radar ambiguity can destabilize mesh estimates,
motivating causal temporal refinement. Rather than buffering
or recurrently processing radar volumes, RaStream processes
each window once and propagates history only through com-
pact token zt and fixed-size states. Because morphology varies
slowly while pose and translation change rapidly, RaStream
separates temporal state into a morphology accumulator ut
and motion tracker ht. This dual-state design refines generic
causal recurrence while preserving responsiveness to current
observations.

coder. Let

C. Initialization and State Recovery
At the beginning of an independent sequence, RaStream
initializes both temporal states to zero. The first valid radar
window is processed by the spatial encoder, after which the
states are updated and predictions are emitted at the chosen
stride. The states are reset when a new sequence or subject
is encountered. If observations are temporarily unavailable,
the most recent states are retained. When the stream resumes,
cached spatial tokens can be replayed to re-warm the temporal
states without recomputing the corresponding volumetric win-
dows. This recovery procedure keeps spatial recomputation
separate from temporal state recovery.

E. Temporal Sampling Parameters
RaStream uses three temporal sampling parameters: local
window length Tw, streaming stride s, and finite unroll horizon
T. Unless otherwise stated, Tw = 4. The effective raw-frame
coverage of a finite training or evaluation segment is
Tcov = Tw + (T − 1)s. (28)
Here Tw controls local radar evidence, s controls output
rate, and T controls the finite training unroll and warm-
up/replay horizon. The recurrent states persist during steady-
state inference and are not reset after every T tokens. Thus,
Tcov describes the physical span of the finite segment, not a
limit on recurrent memory.
For example, Tw = 4, T = 16, and s = 2 cover 34 raw
radar frames, or approximately 2.83 s at 12 Hz. Increasing Tw
increases the cost of each spatial-encoder invocation, increas-
ing T extends the supervised temporal span, and increasing s
lowers the output rate while reducing the amortized invocation
frequency. We evaluate the effects of T and s in Section VI,
while fixing Tw = 4 in the reported experiments.
V. DEPLOYMENT-AWARE STREAMING DESIGN
A. Scale Variants
For deployment, RaStream provides Tiny, Small, and Base
spatial-backbone scales as the model-scale variable m. Table II
summarizes the proportional channel scaling; backbone chan-
nels are listed as Stage1/Stage2/Stage3.
Backbone channels scale by 0.5× (tiny), 0.75× (small),
or 1.0× (base), with downstream dimensions scaled propor-
tionally. The variants span a 4× parameter range and a 3×
compute range without architectural redesign. The base model
targets the highest reconstruction quality, the small model
offers an accuracy-efficiency compromise, and the tiny model
supports latency-sensitive or low-power deployments.
B. Hardware and Inference Pipeline
We deploy RaStream on the NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano, a
representative edge computing platform for embedded sensing
and robotics applications. The device features an NVIDIA
Ampere GPU with 1024 CUDA cores and Tensor Cores, 8 GB
of unified memory shared between CPU and GPU, and typical
power consumption of 7-15W under load. The software stack
consists of PyTorch with TensorRT optimization for inference
acceleration.
The deployment pipeline has two computational stages
within RaStream: spatial encoding and dual-state temporal
refinement. The spatial encoder produces the compact token
and accounts for most inference time because it processes
3D radar volumes. The temporal refiner updates persistent
morphology and motion states, applies the token-conditioned
gate, and runs SMPL-X heads. Since the states persist across
frames, RaStream avoids replaying the finite unroll horizon
from scratch at every output.
The system stores a small ring buffer of compact tokens
rather than raw radar volumes. Streaming updates ut and
ht with O(1) temporal cost, minimizing memory traffic on
embedded devices [44]–[46]. If tracking is interrupted or
frames are dropped, states can be re-warmed by replaying
cached tokens without rerunning the spatial encoder; this
replay is lightweight enough for the short warm-up/replay
horizons used in our system.
Profiling uses batch size one. Each run executes 10 warm-
up inferences before measurement, excludes the first measured
frame from steady-state statistics, synchronizes CUDA around
timed GPU stages, and reports mean and 95th-percentile end-
to-end latency over the remaining stream. Power is read from
Jetson VDD IN before and after the inference run and reported
as idle/active power.
C. Computation and State Memory
Let dz denote the spatial-token dimension and dh the
dimension of the motion state. The spatial encoder is evaluated
once for each emitted token and accounts for most of the
per-invocation computation. The temporal refiner processes
only zt and updates fixed-dimensional states, so its recurrent
computation and state memory do not grow with stream
length. The persistent states require O(dz + dh) memory. If a
replay buffer of K tokens is retained, its memory is O(Kdz),
compared with O(KTwHWD) for storing the corresponding
radar windows.
The stride changes the invocation frequency but not the cost
of an individual spatial-encoder call. If Csp(m) and Ctemp(m)
denote the costs of the spatial and temporal branches at model
scale m, the approximate cost per raw radar frame is
Cframe(m, s) ≈

D. Configuration Space
Edge devices have heterogeneous latency, power, memory,
and output-rate requirements. Because radar mesh recovery
uses sparse 4D volumetric tensors, its accuracy-efficiency
tradeoffs differ from RGB-based systems. We therefore make
the main deployment variables explicit.
A deployment configuration is x = (m, Tw, T, s, q), where
m is model scale, Tw is local radar window length, T is the
finite unroll and warm-up/replay horizon, s is streaming stride,
and q is inference precision. The experiments fix Tw = 4 and
q = FP32, and vary m, T, and s. The RaStream (spatial-only)
variant is used as the non-temporal reference.

B. Training and Inference Protocol
The RaStream spatial encoder is trained first with the single-
window objective described in Section III, using synchronized
radar–mesh pairs. After training, the selected spatial check-
point is applied to the radar stream to produce the compact
tokens used by the causal temporal refiner. The refiner is
trained on finite token segments with the temporal objective in
Section IV. The recurrent states are initialized at the beginning
of each sampled segment during training. At inference time,
the states are carried from one emitted prediction to the next
and reset when a new sequence starts; no future radar frames
are used. The stride determines when a prediction is emitted.
During steady-state inference, ut and ht carry the temporal
context; cached tokens are retained only for warm-up or state
recovery after an interruption.
Training details. Implementation details follow the config-
uration used for the reported runs. The spatial encoder uses
batch size 64, learning rate 2 × 10−4
, three epochs, and step
decay with factor 0.8. Its loss weights are 0.3 for shape, 15
for body pose, 1 for root orientation, 10 for translation and
center anchoring, and 0.5 for gender; vertex and joint losses
are disabled during training and used only for evaluation.
The causal temporal refiner uses cached spatial tokens, batch
size 256, 20 epochs, AdamW with learning rate 3 × 10−4
and weight decay 10−4
, a two-layer GRU motion state with
hidden dimension 512, and zero-initialized recurrent states at
the start of each sampled segment. The temporal loss weights
are 0.3 for all-frame supervision, 0.5 for translation velocity,
0.1 for pose velocity, 0.1 for root-orientation velocity, 0.1 for
translation acceleration, 0.1 for shape consistency, and 0.01
for shape-state smoothness. The best checkpoint is selected
by validation loss.
Baseline protocol. The external baseline numbers in Ta-
ble III follow the M4Human mesh-recovery benchmark pro-
tocol. Methods whose original papers target keypoints, de-
tection, segmentation, or point-cloud sequence representation
are evaluated through the benchmark’s adapted SMPL/SMPL-
X regression setting, so MVE, MJE, MRE, and TE are
computed from reconstructed parametric meshes rather than
from their original task outputs alone. Since existing radar-
tensor SMPL-X baselines are predominantly frame-wise, we
separate the evaluation into two stages: the RaStream (spatial-
only) variants are compared with prior methods under the
common single-window protocol, and the complete framework
is evaluated through controlled causal refinements using the
same pretrained spatial backbone. The dual-state design is
compared with a single-state recurrent baseline.
