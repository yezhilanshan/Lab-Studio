Abstract
Millimeter-wave (mmWave) radar has emerged as a compelling
modality for privacy-preserving Human Pose Estimation (HPE).
However, a fundamental disconnect persists: while real-world do-
mestic environments are inherently multi-user and cluttered, exist-
ing multi-user systems remain predicated on idealized, open spaces.
Through a rigorous empirical study, we expose a critical vulner-
ability in multi-person scenarios: state-of-the-art methods suffer
catastrophic degradation, with the Mean Per Joint Position Error
(MPJPE) spiking to 17.4 cm. Given that this magnitude approaches
the length of a human forearm, such errors inevitably lead to se-
vere skeletal distortion and limb misalignment, rendering the esti-
mated poses structurally incoherent. This scalability barrier stems
from three fundamental physical conflicts: inter-person resolution
bin collisions, severe multipath ghosting, and complex occlusions.
To break this barrier, we present MuPose, the first mmWave sys-
tem tailored for robust multi-person HPE in the wild. By integrat-
ing advanced signal processing with a deep learning backend, Mu-
Pose introduces a hybrid architecture tackling signal chaos via three
innovations: signal-level Null Steering to resolve bin collisions at
the source, AoA-AoD reciprocity to purge multipath ghosts, and
a masked Spatio-Temporal Transformer to infer occluded joints.
Extensive evaluations with 23 subjects across 7 complex environ-
ments demonstrate that MuPose achieves an MPJPE of 6.7 cm, sig-
nificantly outperforming SOTA baselines (an MPJPE of 17.4 cm)
and enabling precise, ubiquitous sensing in domestic settings.

1 Introduction
Human Pose Estimation (HPE) is a cornerstone for a wide range
of applications, spanning activity recognition [58, 66], rehabilita-
tion [22, 34], and virtual reality [18, 107]. To enable these applica-
tions, the researchers have explored multiple sensing modalities,
primarily including vision [42, 64], wearables [35, 108], and wire-
less signals [17, 51, 87, 103–105]. However, vision-based systems
face severe privacy concerns, especially in private spaces, while
wearables are often cumbersome due to their reliance on strict
wearing compliance. Consequently, wireless sensing has emerged
as a compelling alternative, prized for its device-free nature and
inherent privacy preservation [25, 50, 87]. In the realm of wireless
sensing, mmWave radar outperforms alternatives such as WiFi by
leveraging its ultra-wide bandwidth to achieve higher spatial reso-
lution [88, 93], which has catalyzed a wave of mmWave-based HPE
systems [17, 25, 28, 29, 72, 85, 87, 99].
However, a fundamental disconnect remains: while most sys-
tems are optimized for a single subject, real-world domestic en-
vironments are inherently multi-person. Although recent pioneer-
ing works—such as 𝑚3𝑇𝑟𝑎𝑐𝑘 [25], 𝑀4𝑀𝑒𝑠ℎ [85], and 𝑂𝑐𝑐𝑀𝑒𝑠ℎ [6]—
have attempted to address multi-person pose estimation, they largely
assume open, furniture-free spaces, overlooking the harsh constraints
of real homes. As a result, they often break down in complex indoor
settings, where confined layouts and clutter induce inter-person

signal entanglement, severe multipath interference, and frequent
occlusions. To empirically assess state-of-the-art (SOTA) multi-user
systems in the wild, we conducted a pilot field study with 23 sub-
jects across 7 real environments. The results are telling: existing
systems struggle substantially, yielding an MPJPE of 17.4 cm. Even
in moderately complex three-person scenarios, the error remains
high at 15.7 cm. Given that this magnitude approaches the length
of a human forearm, such errors cause severe skeletal distortion,
rendering applications—from fitness coaching to rehabilitation—
infeasible. Thus, building a robust multi-person system remains an
urgent priority.
To achieve mmWave-based multi-person HPE in real-world do-
mestic settings, we must overcome three key challenges:
(i) Inter-person Signal Entanglement: In indoor multi-person
scenarios, signal distinguishability becomes a critical bottleneck.
Current methods typically assume that different users are separa-
ble on the Range-Doppler map or within the point clouds, and sub-
sequently employ algorithms such as clustering based on spatial
locations to separate distinct human bodies [6, 25, 85]. This assump-
tion often fails in confined spaces. Due to the poor angular reso-
lution of commercial radars, existing point generation pipelines
are universally based on distinguishing targets within the Range-
Doppler profile [6, 85, 88]. A common failure mode is Range-Doppler
collision: when two targets (e.g., two torsos) share the same range
and radial velocity, they fall into the same RD bin and become indis-
tinguishable. Crucially, such collisions drastically reduce the den-
sity of the generated point clouds. As reflection peaks merge or
cancel out, valid anatomical cues are lost, producing sparse and
fragmented representations, as shown in Fig. 1. Such collisions
are frequent in real-world settings, causing catastrophic failures
in conventional pipelines.
(ii) Multipath Interference: Indoor environments introduce an-
other formidable hurdle: multipath interference due to cascading
reflections. While static clutter can be removed relatively easily,
dynamic multipath persists. We identify two dominant categories:
(1) inter-person multipath, where signals propagate along a Radar-
Person1-Person2-Radar path; and (2) person-environment multi-
path, where signals travel along a Radar-Person-Object-Radar path
(e.g., furniture). Critically, these dynamic reflections exhibit Doppler
signatures similar to those of moving subjects, making them diffi-
cult to filter. The result is severe ghosting and false targets (Fig. 1).
Most existing multipath suppression techniques rely on static en-
vironmental priors [11, 38], which limits portability across diverse
homes.
(iii) Occlusion: A final challenge arises from occlusion, which
presents a dual threat in real homes: static blockage caused by fur-
niture layouts and dynamic mutual occlusion during multi-person
interactions. Consequently, the radar often captures only partial
human bodies, resulting in inincomplete point clouds. This loss of
spatial information makes reconstructing full skeletal poses highly
ambiguous. While OccMesh leverages human topological priors to
address occlusion [6], it is primarily validated in open spaces and
can struggle in realistic indoor scenes with heavy obstruction.
To address these challenges, we present MuPose, the first mmWave
system designed to break the scalability barrier of multi-user HPE

generation of high-fidelity point clouds.
(ii) AoA-AoD Ghost Suppression: To eliminate multipath inter-
ference, we leverage the geometric reciprocity between Angle of
Departure (AoD) and Angle of Arrival (AoA) [24, 62]. Our approach
is grounded in a fundamental symmetry: direct Line-of-Sight re-
flections satisfy the condition 𝐴𝑜𝐴 ≈ 𝐴𝑜𝐷, whereas multipath
reflections—distorted by environmental bounces—break this con-
straint, as illustrated in Fig. 2(b). However, directly applying this
geometric metric to dense, multi-person point clouds is prone to se-
vere match ambiguity. To overcome this, we introduce an Inverse
Synthetic Aperture Radar (ISAR) decomposition step to resolve the
human body into discrete scattering centers. This enables us to
strictly evaluate AoA-AoD consistency at the individual scatterer
level, effectively filtering out artifacts to reconstruct multipath-
free human representations.
(iii) Masked Occlusion Recovery: Finally, we tackle occlusion
via a Masked Spatio-Temporal Transformer. Our core insight is
that human motion follows strict topological and kinematic con-
straints, allowing us to hallucinate missing body parts from partial
observations. We employ a Point-Transformer to capture these spa-
tial priors, inferring the complete skeleton from visible segments.
To further resolve persistent blockages, we integrate a temporal
Transformer that exploits motion continuity, recognizing that body

Adopting a signal processing-to-AI architecture, we orchestrate

physics-driven frontend, we re-architect the standard point cloud

the synthesis of multipath-free, high-density point clouds. Then,
our AI-driven backend, a Masked Spatio-Temporal Transformer,

We implement MuPose on commercial mmWave radar platforms.
For a comprehensive evaluation, we conducted a large-scale data
collection campaign involving 23 subjects across 7 diverse real-
world environments, totaling over 17.9 hours of data. Experimen-
tal results show that MuPose achieves 6.7 cm MPJPE in confined,
multipath-rich scenarios (supporting up to 6 subjects), significantly
outperforming SOTA baselines (an MPJPE of 17.4 cm). Our contri-
butions are summarized as follows:
• We propose MuPose, an mmWave system that significantly ad-
vances robust, high-fidelity multi-person pose estimation in real-
istic, multipath-rich domestic environments.
• We present the first framework to achieve dense, multipath-free
multi-person point cloud generation. By redesigning the extrac-
tion workflow, we leverage null steering to disentangle user sig-
nals and AoA-AoD reciprocity to eliminate complex indoor mul-
tipath artifacts.
• We introduce a masked spatio-temporal transformer designed to
tackle severe occlusion, achieving robust and precise pose esti-
mation for multiple subjects.
• We implement MuPose on COTS radar and validate it via a large-
scale field study across 7 complex environments. Results demon-
strate an MPJPE of 6.7 cm, significantly outperforming existing
SOTA baselines.
2 Background and Motivation
This section reviews existing approaches to multi-user HPE, ana-
lyzes their fundamental limitations in real-world indoor environ-
ments, and motivates our system by empirical study.
2.1 Existing Work on Multi-User HPE
Research on mmWave-based human pose estimation has surged,
yet mostly targets single user [17, 28, 29, 31, 52, 72, 80, 87, 92]. Re-
cent efforts—such as 𝑚3Track [25], 𝑀4Mesh [85], and OccMesh
[6]—have begun pushing toward multi-person sensing. However,
these systems are predominantly evaluated in pristine, open envi-
ronments and largely overlook the harsh constraints of real homes.
Specifically, 𝑚3Track separates users based on Range–Doppler and
Range–Angle profiles, but it does not address the severe occlusion
and multipath interference that are pervasive in indoor settings.
𝑀4Mesh segments multi-user point clouds through spatial parti-
tioning before pose regression, yet it remains vulnerable to point
cloud sparsity induced by RD-bin collisions, corruption from multi-
path ghosts, and strong occlusion. OccMesh also operates on point
clouds and leverages human topological priors to mitigate occlu-
sion; while highly effective in open spaces, it struggles under re-
alistic indoor conditions characterized by RD collisions, multipath
reflections, and heavy environmental obstruction.

3 MuPose Design
Before we delve deeper into MuPose, we present the design prin-
ciples that underpin our system. Our framework is positioned as a
synergistic signal processing and AI system.
3.1 Design Outline.
(i) Physically-Informed Signal Processing. We first leverage
advanced signal processing to reconstruct high density, low in-
terference point clouds, followed by deep learning models to es-
timate multi-person poses. The rationale lies in the distinct nature
of these two tasks. The transformation from raw radar signals to
point clouds is governed by explicit mathematical derivations and
wave propagation laws; it is a deterministic process best handled
by rigorous signal processing algorithms to ensure precision and
interpretability. Accordingly, we address both inter-person signal
entanglement and multipath interference via explicit signal pro-
cessing formulations.
(ii) Data-Driven Pose Estimation. Conversely, estimating poses
from point clouds is an ill-posed inverse problem: it requires in-
ferring complete, structured skeletal kinematics from sparse, un-
structured, and noisy observations. This task necessitates the use
of implicit topological priors, which are more effectively modeled
by data-driven deep learning. Accordingly, our solution for occlu-
sion relies on a Spatio Temporal Transformer, designed specifically
to capture these implicit spatial and kinematic constraints.
(iii) Point Cloud over Heatmap. MmWave HPE methods can be
roughly divided by their network input into heatmap-based and
point-cloud-based designs. While heatmaps capture all raw reflec-
tions, they are plagued by noise and multipath, resulting in a low
SNR. Point clouds, conversely, distill high-value information into a
compact format, albeit with the risk of missing weaker signals. We
adopt a point-cloud-based formulation for two key reasons. First,
point clouds are intuitive and human-interpretable: one can visu-
ally inspect a point cloud and immediately gauge its quality and
whether it is sufficient for pose estimation, which is far less fea-
sible with heatmaps. Second, we build on our prior ETCM-CFAR
design [88], which effectively recovers weak reflections that stan-
dard methods might miss.

3.3 Multipath-Free Coarse Point Extraction
This module aims to extract valid reflection points from the gener-
ated RD maps while rigorously filtering out environmental ghosts.
However, due to inherent signal overlaps in the Range-Doppler do-
main, the output of this stage remains ”coarse,” serving primarily
as the angular bootstrap for the subsequent fine-grained genera-
tion.
3.3.1 Combating Energy Masking via ETCM-CFAR. Conventional
spatial-based CFAR treats targets as points. However, humans act
as extended targets in short-range sensing, causing reference cells
to overlap with the body. This triggers energy masking—an over-
estimation of noise that suppresses valid detections. We solve this
via ETCM-CFAR [88]. By shifting noise estimation to the tempo-
ral domain, it circumvents spatial masking, effectively recovering
dense anatomical reflections that are otherwise discarded.
3.3.2 Ghost Elimination via AoA-AoD Reciprocity. A critical chal-
lenge in indoor sensing is dynamic multipath interference. To purge

3.4 Fine-Grained Points via Null Steering
To fundamentally resolve Inter-person Signal Entanglement, we in-
troduce a software-defined Null Steering mechanism. Our objec-
tive is to spatially filter the signal on the Range-Doppler (RD) spec-
trum, steering the main lobe toward the target subject while plac-
ing deep nulls in the directions of interference. We systematize this
process into four key stages.
3.4.1 Angular Prior Extraction. The first challenge is to determine
the angular parameters for targets and interference. We leverage
the ”coarse point clouds” generated in the previous stage as spa-
tial priors. Given the hardware limitation of vertical antennas in
commercial radars, we focus exclusively on the azimuth dimen-
sion. Since a human body acts as an extended target, we perform
clustering on the coarse points to decompose each subject into 𝐾
(1 ≤ 𝐾 ≤ 3) distinct scattering centers (e.g., torso, limbs). These
centers serve as the anchor points for our beamforming vectors.
3.4.2 Adaptive Activation Strategy. To optimize computational ef-
ficiency and preserve array degrees of freedom (DoFs), we do not
apply Null Steering blindly. We observe that entanglement only
occurs when subjects share similar radial distances (i.e., Range-
Doppler collisions). Accordingly, we devise a range-based check:
(i) Collision Zone: If the range difference between a target center
and any interference center is within a collision threshold, we ac-
tivate Null Steering to suppress the interference. (ii) Safe Zone: If
targets are well-separated in range, we revert to standard Beam-
forming to maximize the Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) and conserve
resources.

on our prior work mmNull [97], we formulate this as an optimiza-

3.5 Spatio-Temporal Pose Estimation
The final stage of MuPose transforms the disentangled, high-density
point clouds into continuous skeletal kinematics. While the physics-
driven modules in the previous stages ensure that the point clouds
are subject-exclusive and free of multipath artifacts, inferring a
full-body skeleton from surface reflections remains an inherently
ill-posed inverse problem. Millimeter-wave point clouds, even when
enhanced, only capture partial human contours and are highly sus-
ceptible to occlusion by furniture or self-blocking. To address this
pervasive challenge, we design a Masked Spatio-Temporal Trans-
former, as illustrated in Fig. 11. This architecture is engineered
to ”hallucinate” missing body parts by explicitly capturing two
fundamental priors: structural anatomical constraints and tempo-
ral motion continuity. Furthermore, we employ a masked training
strategy to enforce the network’s robustness against severe signal
dropouts.
3.5.1 Spatial Recovery via Spatial Transformer. To extract geomet-
ric features from unstructured point sets, we employ a Point Trans-
former encoder [101] for each frame. The network initially maps
the raw physical features (e.g., 3D coordinates and Doppler veloc-
ity) into a compact 32-dimensional latent space. We purposefully
keep this initial point-level dimension lightweight to prevent over-
fitting to the extremely sparse and noisy radar inputs, while mini-
mizing computational overhead.
A critical design choice in this module is the feature aggregation
mechanism. Traditional point cloud networks often rely on global
max-pooling, which indiscriminately collapses local geometric de-
tails. To overcome this, we introduce Frame-wise Attention Pool-
ing parameterized by 𝑄 = 4 learnable queries. We empirically set
𝑄 = 4 to strike a balance between efficiency and semantic granu-
larity, allowing the queries to implicitly attend to major anatomical
regions (e.g., torso, head, upper limbs, and lower limbs). Guided by
the skeletal ground truth, this attention mechanism dynamically
aggregates the dense 32-dimensional point features into semantic
tokens. Crucially, to accommodate the complex reasoning required
in subsequent stages, these tokens are then linearly projected into
a highly expressive 256-dimensional space. Consequently, the spa-
tial module outputs four 256-dimensional tokens per frame, suc-
cessfully encoding spatial anatomical priors and inferring implicit
body structures from fragmented observations.

3.5.2 Temporal Kinematic Modeling. In realistic indoor environ-
ments, spatial information in a single frame is often insufficient to
resolve ambiguities caused by transient occlusions or signal fading.
To mitigate this, we integrate a Temporal Transformer designed to
capture long-range motion dynamics across frames.
Taking the sequence of 256-dimensional spatial tokens as in-
put, the temporal model is configured with a hidden dimension
of 𝑑𝑚𝑜𝑑𝑒𝑙 = 256 and a stack of 𝐿 = 4 encoder layers. This spe-
cific depth is optimized to provide sufficient reasoning capacity for
non-linear human motions without introducing exorbitant latency.
Each layer features Multi-Head Self-Attention (𝐻 = 4, dropout=0.1)
to capture inter-frame correlations. To explicitly preserve the se-
quential order of movement—a fundamental property of kinematics—
we inject sinusoidal positional encodings into the feature stream.
By aggregating contextual cues across a sliding time window, the
Temporal Transformer effectively learns biomechanical kinematic
priors (e.g., velocity smoothness and inertia). This ensures that the
estimated pose sequences bridge the gaps caused by temporary sig-
nal loss and adhere to physical laws, effectively eliminating unre-
alistic skeletal jitter.
3.5.3 Query-based Keypoint Decoding. To decode explicit skele-
tal coordinates from the high-dimensional spatiotemporal mem-
ory, we depart from traditional heatmap-based estimation, which
suffers from quantization errors and high computational overhead.
Instead, we employ a DETR-style Transformer Decoder [7, 30] for
direct, continuous coordinate regression.
We introduce 𝐾 = 17 learnable joint queries, each semantically
initialized to target a specific anatomical keypoint (e.g., left wrist,
right knee). These queries act as intelligent agents, utilizing cross-
attention mechanisms to dynamically search and aggregate joint-
specific contexts from the global spatiotemporal tokens. This query-
based design is particularly robust against occlusion: even if the
local point cloud for a specific limb is entirely missing, the corre-
sponding joint query can still ”hallucinate” its position by attend-
ing to the movement of connected joints and historical frames. Fi-
nally, a shared Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP) head projects each
updated query directly to its 3D coordinate (𝑥, 𝑦, 𝑧), achieving an
elegant, end-to-end pose estimation pipeline.
3.5.4 Physics-Informed Loss Function. To enforce anatomical plau-
sibility, we design a composite loss function that explicitly incor-
porates geometric and kinematic constraints:
𝐿𝑡𝑜𝑡𝑎𝑙 = 𝐿𝑝𝑜𝑠 + 𝜆1𝐿𝑏𝑜𝑛𝑒 + 𝜆2𝐿𝑡𝑒𝑚𝑝 (6)
Here, 𝐿𝑝𝑜𝑠 minimizes the MPJPE, 𝐿𝑏𝑜𝑛𝑒 penalizes deviations from
fixed bone lengths to prevent unnatural skeletal stretching, 𝐿𝑡𝑒𝑚𝑝
enforces motion smoothness between adjacent frames to mitigate
high-frequency jitter [12, 47, 59]. This approach ensures that the
inferred poses are not just statistically likely, but biologically plau-
sible.
3.5.5 Robust Data Augmentation Strategy. To ensure generaliza-
tion to complex indoor environments, we implement a comprehen-
sive Data Augmentation Strategy. We randomly mask a significant
percentage of input points during training. This adversarial-style
training compels the model to master global structural reasoning
rather than relying on local features. To enforce view and scale
invariance, we apply random rotations (around the vertical axis)
and scaling/translation to the input point clouds. This prevents
the model from overfitting to specific sensor placements or sub-
ject sizes.
3.5.6 Lightweight Design. To enable real-time edge inference, we
eliminate spatiotemporal redundancy. Spatially, we adopt Grouped
Local Vector Attention, reducing complexity from quadratic 𝑂(𝑁2)
to linear 𝑂(𝑁 ×𝑘). Temporally, we implement a FIFO Feature Buffer
to bypass redundant sliding-window computations. By executing
the Spatial Transformer only on the incoming frame and retriev-
ing cached history, we decouple spatial extraction from temporal
modeling. This reduces per-frame cost by a factor of the window
size 𝑇.

of 3.75 cm and a velocity resolution of 5 cm/s. The system oper-

were employed to capture skeletal ground truth. To verify cross-
platform portability, we further validated the pipeline on 77 GHz

5.5 System Latency and Efficiency
To validate real-time feasibility, we evaluated MuPose on a laptop
with an NVIDIA RTX 5090 GPU, achieving a total end-to-end la-
tency of 58.4 ms per frame (≈17 FPS). This high efficiency stems
from three synergistic optimizations. First, regarding algorithmic
acceleration, we replace traditional spatial-window CFAR with our
temporal-based ETCM-CFAR [88]. This reduces the time complex-
ity of point extraction by nearly 100×, effectively eliminating the
primary bottleneck in the signal processing frontend. Second, we
implement redundancy elimination in the Null Steering module
via an Iterative Refinement strategy(Sec. 3.4). By processing only
newly emerged peaks rather than recalculating the entire spec-
trum, we avoid redundant beamforming operations. Finally, we

6 Related Work
Wireless Sensing. Driven by the intrinsic privacy-preserving na-
ture of RF signals, the community has witnessed a surge in re-
search across diverse sensing modalities, including mmWave radar
[8, 9, 15, 39, 79, 106], Wi-Fi [32, 33, 41, 68, 70, 71, 89–91], LoRa
[49, 81, 82], RFID [2, 3, 53, 54, 60, 61, 83], acoustic sensing [16, 40],
and UWB [36, 37, 73]. These technologies have unlocked a myriad
of ubiquitous applications, spanning from fine-grained vital sign
monitoring [1, 19, 102], activity and gesture recognition [23, 69, 98],
and human tracking [43, 46, 77], to broader environmental percep-
tion tasks such as imaging [4, 5, 14, 26, 55, 75, 76], drone sensing
[21, 27, 67], vibration analysis [44, 84], and even soil monitoring
[13].
MmWave Sensing. Originally rooted in autonomous driving [56,
57, 65], mmWave radar has been democratized by semiconductor
advances. With single-chip costs dropping to $2, large-scale house-
hold deployment is now viable. This accessibility has catalyzed
human sensing research, spanning micro-motion tasks like vital
signs [10, 95], to macro-motion applications such as tracking [25,
78], gesture recognition [20, 45], pose estimation [17, 72, 86, 87],
and activity recognition [48, 74, 93, 94, 96]. The latter fundamen-
tally relies on high-quality point clouds, which provide a light-
weight yet expressive representation of dynamic postures [85, 87,
88, 100]. However, acquiring clean indoor point clouds is severely
bottlenecked by multipath interference. While prior efforts utilize
AoA-AoD reciprocity for multipath filtering [24, 62], extending
this to refine dense mmWave point clouds in multi-user scenarios
remains largely unexplored.
7 Discussion
Range Constraints. Our evaluation identifies an operational bound-
ary at approximately 5 meters, beyond which skeletal reconstruc-
tion fidelity declines. This is an inevitable consequence of the inverse-
square law, where radar echo power diminishes rapidly with dis-
tance, leading to sparse point clouds and low SNR. Despite this,
the current 5m range adequately satisfies the spatial requirements
for most indoor human sensing tasks. To scale MuPose to expan-
sive environments, we envision a multi-node deployment strategy,
where handed-over tracking between distributed radars can ex-
tend the effective sensing envelope indefinitely.
Maximum Subject Support. Our evaluation demonstrates that
MuPose robustly supports up to 5 individuals while maintaining
sub-10cm accuracy. This capacity is well-aligned with the demo-
graphics of standard households, effectively covering daily fam-
ily activities. As the crowd size increases to 6 subjects, the sys-
tem encounters a performance inflection point (g-MPJPE ≈ 12.5
cm). While this accuracy is sufficient for basic presence detection,
it marks the limit for fine-grained skeletal reconstruction with a
single device. Future work will explore distributed radar arrays to
resolve such hyper-dense scenarios.
Towards End-to-End Multi-Person Estimation. Our current
pipeline handles multi-person scenarios by separating users via
rule-based spatial clustering prior to individual pose regression.
While effective in standard settings, this purely spatial heuristic
can occasionally introduce errors when users are in close proxim-
ity or actively interacting. In such dense environments, the cluster-
ing algorithm may sometimes struggle to distinguish overlapping
reflections, erroneously merging distinct users or fragmenting a
single individual’s point cloud. These segmentation inaccuracies
can inadvertently propagate irreversible geometric errors to the
downstream pose estimator, leading to potential identity switches
or degraded kinematic accuracy. To fundamentally address these
limitations, our future work aims to transition towards a fully end-
to-end multi-person pose estimation framework. By replacing the
heuristic clustering step with a unified neural architecture, the
system can directly leverage global spatiotemporal context and
anatomical priors to jointly resolve user identities and kinematics,
achieving highly robust perception even in crowded indoor spaces.
Beyond Pose Estimation: Towards Applications and Interac-
tive Agents. While MuPose achieves robust multi-person pose es-
timation, we view this as a foundational building block rather than
the end goal. Building upon these high-fidelity skeletal sequences,
our immediate future work focuses on fine-grained activity recog-
nition and clinical gait analysis to non-intrusively monitor motor-
related conditions, such as Parkinson’s disease. Ultimately, we plan
to bridge the gap between physical sensing and semantic under-
standing by integrating MuPose with Large Language Models (LLMs)
to construct an interactive mmWave Agent. By grounding LLM
reasoning in continuous spatial kinematics, this multimodal agent
will not only comprehend complex human behaviors and perform
high-level semantic reasoning, but also engage in natural, context-
aware interactions with users, unlocking a new frontier for privacy-
preserving human-computer interaction in smart environments.
8 Conclusion
We present MuPose, the first mmWave Human Pose Estimation
system to overcome the multi-user scalability barrier in complex
environments. By integrating physics-driven signal disentangle-
ment (Null Steering and AoA-AoD reciprocity) with an AI-driven
Masked Spatio-Temporal Transformer, MuPose fundamentally re-
solves severe multipath and occlusion challenges. Evaluations show
MuPose achieves a robust MPJPE of 6.7 cm, outperforming current
baselines (17.4 cm) by over 60% and enabling practical, privacy-
preserving multi-person sensing.
