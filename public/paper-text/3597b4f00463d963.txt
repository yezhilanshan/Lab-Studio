ABSTRACT The wireless channel is fundamental to communication, encompassing numerous tasks
collectively referred to as channel-associated tasks. These tasks can leverage joint learning based on
channel characteristics to share representations and enhance system design. To capitalize on this advantage,
LLM4WM is proposed—a large language model (LLM) multi-task fine-tuning framework specifically
tailored for channel-associated tasks. This framework utilizes a Mixture of Experts with Low-Rank Adap-
tation (MoE-LoRA) approach for multi-task fine-tuning, enabling the transfer of the pre-trained LLM’s
general knowledge to these tasks. Given the unique characteristics of wireless channel data, preprocessing
modules, adapter modules, and multi-task output layers are designed to align the channel data with the
LLM’s semantic feature space. Experiments on a channel-associated multi-task dataset demonstrate that
LLM4WM outperforms existing methodologies in both full-sample and few-shot evaluations, owing to its
robust multi-task joint modeling and transfer learning capabilities.

I. INTRODUCTION
THE wireless channel plays a critical role in deter-
mining the quality and reliability of communication.
To achieve low latency and high reliability, millimeter-wave
(mmWave) and Multiple-Input Multiple-Output (MIMO)
technologies are among the most promising solutions [1],
[2], [3]. Maximizing the benefits of these technologies relies
heavily on accurate channel estimation [4]. However, this task
becomes increasingly challenging as the number of antennas
grows and environmental dynamics become more complex.
Understanding the wireless channel’s characteristics—such
as fading, interference, and multipath propagation—is crucial
for optimizing communication performance, and also sup-
ports other related technologies, such as the design of
integrated sensing and communications (ISAC) systems [5].
Fortunately, artificial intelligence (AI) has significantly
improved channel estimation accuracy, fostering optimism
for the practical implementation of advanced wireless appli-
cations [6], [7], [8]. AI also enhances various communi-
cation tasks, including channel prediction, beamforming,
and positioning, demonstrating notable effectiveness and
robustness.
Although AI has demonstrated significant potential in
communication systems, existing AI-powered communication

methods still encounter several issues. First, AI approaches

issues, AI models need to be retrained in response to dynamic

to the limited scale of the models.
To address these challenges, [9] proposed that multi-modal
sensing could effectively capture the propagation charac-
teristics of the wireless channel, a concept summarized as
the Synesthesia of Machines (SoM). This approach aims to
optimize communication systems by leveraging multi-modal
sensing to enhance their design and performance [10], [11].
Another potential optimization approach is the introduc-
tion of multi-task learning (MTL). Given that the wireless
channel is a critical component of the wireless communica-
tion process, and many communication tasks focus on the
extraction and utilization of channel features under various
conditions, jointly learning these channel-associated tasks
can yield significant training benefits by extracting shared
channel representations across tasks. For instance, in [12],
the joint training and inference of direct channel and cas-
caded channel estimation in reconfigurable intelligent surface
systems were proposed, effectively reducing pilot overhead
and improving model performance. While effective to some
extent, these methods encounter limitations. In particular,
sharing representations across tasks can lead to performance
trade-offs, where enhancing one task may degrade another.
Moreover, the limited model capacity restricts scalability to
more diversified tasks, as current approaches are typically
constrained to pairs of similar tasks.
Recently, Large language models (LLMs) have become
powerful learners adept at handling multiple tasks, demon-
strating remarkable reasoning capabilities and generalization
across various domains, including natural language process-
ing (NLP) [13], healthcare [14], law [15], and finance [16].
The GPT-4 has shown outstanding performance in NLP tasks,
while TTM [17] has also demonstrated remarkable few-shot
and zero-shot learning abilities in time series processing
tasks, meaning it can generalize to new tasks with only
a few or even no task-specific training samples. Inspired
by these successes, there is growing interest in leveraging
pre-trained large models for cross-domain tasks, including
channel-associated tasks in wireless communication. For
instance, an LLM-empowered channel prediction method
LLM4CP was proposed in [18], achieving greatly improved
few-shot generalization ability. Reference [19] similarly
exploits the strong predictive capacity of pre-trained large
models, achieving competitive performance in the beam
prediction task. However, these approaches are limited to
single-task settings and fall short of harnessing the native
multi-task potential of pre-trained large models. Our objec-
tive is to enhance multiple channel-associated tasks in wire-
less communication using LLMs, which poses challenges
such as effectively transferring cross-domain knowledge
and managing task diversity. We address these challenges
through the MoE-LoRA fine-tuning method [20], designed
for multi-task scenarios, while noting that recent work has
not sufficiently considered the specific relationships between
channel-associated tasks, which may limit the model’s capac-
ity to acquire general representations.
Specifically, we propose a wireless multi-task fine-tuning
framework leveraging pre-trained LLMs, which is success-
fully applied to the channel-associated multi-tasks. Unlike
previous MTL approaches that rely on shared bottom layers,
we freeze most of the large model’s parameters and introduce
MoE-LoRA for wireless multi-task fine-tuning. On the one
hand, tasks can share experts’ weight, which helps the net-
work learn common knowledge across tasks, while on the
other hand, the independence of experts and the gating mech-
anism ensure the differentiation of task-specific features.
Additionally, we incorporate multi-task adapter modules at
both the input and output layers of the LLM to ensure align-
ment between the feature space of communication tasks and

II. RELATED WORKS
A. AI FOR WIRELESS COMMUNICATIONS PHYSICAL
LAYER
Benefiting from its powerful representational capabilities,
AI has demonstrated outstanding performance across various
tasks in the physical layer of wireless communications [21],
[22], [23]. In scenarios with severe interference or highly
dynamic environments, traditional model-based approaches
often struggle to maintain communication quality due to
limited modeling accuracy and their inability to capture
complex environmental dynamics. In contrast, AI-driven
methods, which rely on data-driven learning, significantly
enhance performance under such challenging conditions.
However, existing lightweight AI models often suffer from
poor generalization and limited task universality, hindering
their practical deployment.

B. MULTI-TASK LEARNING IN WIRELESS
COMMUNICATIONS
MTL effectively captures task relatedness and enables
the learning of shared representations across tasks,
thereby improving both task-specific performance and
generalization. This capability has led to its widespread adop-
tion in computer vision [24] and natural language processing.
Similarly, wireless communication tasks exhibit varying
degrees of inter-task correlation that can be exploited. For
example, in [25], two key tasks of wireless signal recog-
nition, signal classification, and modulation recognition,
were jointly trained to achieve performance gains through
task synergy. Nevertheless, existing MTL efforts in wireless
communication mainly focus on closely related tasks and
struggle to generalize across tasks with weak or negligible
correlations [26]. As a result, most prior works are limited in
scale, often involving only two tasks [12], which restricts the
full potential of MTL. To address this, we consider six diverse
yet channel-associated tasks that vary in both data structure
and task formats, creating a sufficiently challenging setting.
By leveraging the strong modeling capacity of large models,
we successfully achieve joint learning across all six tasks and
observe significant multi-task synergy gains.
C. LLM FOR WIRELESS COMMUNICATIONS
LLMs have emerged as transformative tools across numerous
domains. Leveraging LLMs to enhance wireless communica-
tion systems has become an increasingly prominent area of
research [27], [28], [29], [30]. On one hand, prompt-based
methods apply LLMs to language-oriented tasks such as
software log analysis [31], and transceiver configuration [32].
On the other hand, fine-tuning techniques have been
employed to adapt LLMs for non-language-oriented tasks.
For instance, NetLLM [33] fine-tunes Llama2 with LoRA to
perform network-layer tasks effectively. However, NetLLM
treats each task independently without leveraging inter-
task correlations, leading to suboptimal performance and
increased training costs. Recent works have also begun to
explore the use of LLMs in physical layer tasks, which
impose stricter latency constraints and pose greater semantic
gaps from language models [18], [19]. In [34], an LLM-
based MTL framework is proposed for physical-layer tasks,
but their method ignores task relationships, which degrades
performance. To address these shortcomings, we propose a
carefully designed MoE-LoRA-based multi-task fine-tuning
framework that mitigates task interference and effectively
harnesses multi-task synergy.

orthogonal frequency division multiplexing (OFDM) tech-
nologies applied to each. The sub-6G and mmWave antennas

IV. TASK DESCRIPTION
To achieve higher SE in the sub-6G frequency band, accurate
channel matrix estimation from pilot signals is essential [6].
Research has focused on enhancing pilot-based channel
estimation for massive antenna arrays and addressing chan-
nel aging in high mobility scenarios through time-domain
prediction [22]. Additionally, some studies reduce pilot over-
head by extrapolating in the frequency domain [23]. In the
mmWave frequency band, the increased number of antennas
necessitates improved beam scanning efficiency. Researchers
utilize the spatial correlation between frequency bands to
estimate optimal mmWave beams based on the sub-6G chan-
nel matrix [36]. Parameters like user distance xd and path
loss xpl are also critical for configuring communication
links, with some works estimating these factors from chan-
nel data [37], [38]. Although the objectives and optimization
methods of these tasks differ, they all leverage characteristics
of sub-6 GHz channels. Moreover, BS often needs to perform
these tasks simultaneously for efficient communication sys-
tem operation. Thus, designing a unified network to address
these tasks concurrently is essential.
We categorize these tasks into three classes: channel
reconstruction, beam management, and radio environment
mining. To enhance clarity and understanding, we define
T = {CE, CP, PF, BF, DE, PE} as the task set. The detailed

B. MULTI-TASK ADAPTER MODULE
We extend the conventional use of adapter modules, which
incorporate a small number of trainable parameters, enabling
the model to preserve its existing modeling and generalization
capabilities while adapting to a specific domain [39], [40].
However, it is primarily designed for single-task scenarios
and lacks the ability to facilitate transfer generalization across
multiple tasks simultaneously.
Unlike existing single-task adapters, the multi-task adapter

E. TRAINING CONFIGURATION
The proposed network is trained on a multi-task mixed dataset
using a two-stage training approach. In the first stage, only
the multi-task adapters and output layer are trained, while
the LLM parameters are frozen. At this point, the model
learns the mapping between the task feature space and the
pre-trained LLM’s text feature space. In the second stage,
the LLM is fine-tuned by MoE-LoRA, while the multi-task
adapters become frozen, but the output layer is still trainable.
At this stage, the model leverages the LLM for joint modeling
of multiple tasks, achieving better results by utilizing gener-
alized representations across tasks. The same loss function is

is used as the loss function.

2) BASELINES
To validate the superiority of the proposed method, several
model-based and deep learning-based methods are imple-
mented as baselines.
a: TRADITIONAL METHODS (WITHOUT DEEP LEARNING)
This class of methods does not rely on a training process but
instead leverages the inherent characteristics of the channel
to address specific problems.
• BI: In this method, CSI is treated as a time series,
and bilinear interpolation (BI) is used to complete the
channel reconstruction tasks.
• Codebook [45]: Based on spatial correlation, a super-
resolution codebook is used for beam scanning in the
sub-6G band to obtain the optimal mmWave downlink
beam vector. This method is used to process the beam
management task.
• FIFS [46]: FIFS is a CSI-based fingerprinting system
that introduces a coherence bandwidth-enhanced prob-
ability algorithm, utilizing a correlation filter to map
objects to fingerprints. It is implemented for radio envi-
ronment mining tasks.
b: SINGLE-TASK SMALL MODEL METHODS
This class of methods employs specially designed model
components to address specific downstream tasks, which
often have a relatively small number of parameters.
• MLP [21], [47]: Many works have employed MLP to
model complex mapping relationships in communica-
tion problems. We implement an MLP for the task of
radio environment sensing and beam management.
• LSTM [48]: LSTM is designed with memory cells and
multiplicative gates to deal with long-term dependency.
We implement it using 4 LSTM layers for processing
channel reconstruction tasks.
• CNN [23]: A CNN-based predictor for FDD systems is
proposed in [23], where the prediction of time-frequency
CSI data is treated as a two-dimensional image process-
ing task. It contains ten convolutional layers, where the
convolution kernel size is 3 × 3. We implement it for
processing channel reconstruction tasks.
• WiT [37]: A transformer-based location estimation
method, which leverages the attention mechanism to
achieve robust learning effects. We implement it as
described in [37] for processing radio environment sens-
ing tasks.
c: MULTI-TASK SMALL MODEL METHODS
This class of methods employs techniques such as low-level
sharing and cross-feature fusion to enable feature sharing
across different tasks, thereby achieving the functionality of
a multi-purpose model.
• Cross-stitch [24]: A convolutional multi-task learning
neural network equipped with ‘‘cross-stitch’’ unit, which
could combine the activations from multiple networks.
We implement it by using ResNet [49] as the backbone
layer. To illustrate the impact of wireless multi-task
learning for small model, Cross-stitch(s) is added as
a baseline. We implement it by directly applying the
cross-stitch network while only performing a single task.
d: SINGLE-TASK LARGE MODEL METHODS
This class of methods typically fine-tunes large models for
a single downstream task, achieving strong performance
by leveraging the powerful modeling capabilities of large
models.
• LLM4CP [18]: This method is the first to apply LLM
to the channel prediction task through fine-tuning.
We implement it and choose GPT-2 as the backbone
LLM and LN Tuning [50] as the fine-tuning method for
processing Channel Reconstruction tasks.
• LLM4WM(s): A single-task fine-tuning based large
model network, where we directly apply our proposed
LLM4WM whilst only performing a single task.

1) OVERALL PERFORMANCE
Table 3 shows that LLM4WM outperforms non-learning

leading to performance degradation in challenging scenarios.

generalized performance. Meanwhile, benefiting from the
strong modeling capacity of large models (LM), LLM4WM

models and small models, and find that the small model
suffers an average improvement of only 0.19 dB when switch-
ing from single-task to multi-task learning, while the large
model shows an improvement of 0.99 dB. This is due to
the large model’s ability to extract joint representations,
unlike the small model, which struggles with conflicting task
knowledge. Thus, large models are better suited for handling
wireless multi-task learning.

Generalization, referring to the ability of models to maintain
performance in new communication scenarios, is crucial for
real-world deployment as it reduces the need for frequent

approach consistently outperforms others across most tasks,
achieving average performance gains of 1.33 dB and 1.25 dB,
respectively, compared to the single-task small models. This
improvement is largely attributed to the general knowledge
encoded in large models, enhancing the generalization across
diverse tasks. In contrast, small models, trained on specific
datasets, often lack such diversity, thus performing poorly
in new scenarios. Their limited capacity hinders their ability
to learn abstract, transferable representations, making them
susceptible to overfitting to task-specific patterns. As a result,
once exposed to new configurations, these models struggle to
adapt and result in a significant performance degradation. The
slight dip in radio environment mining performance is due
to the task’s relative simplicity in the LOS scenario, where
smaller models like WiT excel. However, our model excels in
more complex tasks like channel estimation, which requires
understanding multidimensional features, further confirming
that large models are better suited for dynamic real-world
communication scenarios.

VII. CONCLUSION
In this paper, we have proposed a novel multi-task fine-tuning
framework tailored for large models in wireless communi-
cation systems. By leveraging a diverse multi-task dataset,
our approach has enabled the model to perform various
wireless channel-associated tasks concurrently. To facili-
tate the extraction of shared representations across multiple
tasks, we integrated MoE-LoRA into the fine-tuning pro-
cess, empowering the backbone model to dynamically adapt
by optimally combining expert modules and improving
task-specific performance. Additionally, we employed a
multi-task adapter to harmonize the feature spaces of different
tasks with the semantic embedding space of the large model,
ensuring coherent task alignment. Preliminary simulation
results have demonstrated the robust multi-task learning
and generalization capabilities of the proposed LLM4WM
framework. Furthermore, ablation studies have underscored
the critical contributions of each module to overall system
performance. The expert weight heatmap has validated the
efficacy of the MoE mechanism in adaptively allocating
expert resources, highlighting its role in enhancing model
specialization and flexibility.
