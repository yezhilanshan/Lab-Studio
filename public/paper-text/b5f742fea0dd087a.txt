A B S T R A C T
As a non-contact sensing device, millimeter-wave radar exhibits unique strengths in human activity recognition
(HAR). Existing methods rely on micro-Doppler signatures for activity classification, but they often encounter
feature aliasing in multi-person activity recognition (MPAR) scenarios. Although point cloud-based approaches
can distinguish individual targets, they primarily extract static morphological features, neglecting the micro-
motion information of human joints, which is crucial for accurate activity recognition. To address these
limitations, we proposes an innovative MPAR framework that integrates spatial point clouds and micro-motion
features. First, an improved point cloud data association algorithm is applied to achieve multi-target point
cloud feature separation, followed by a dynamic projection mechanism to construct time–Doppler feature
maps. Then, a torso micro-motion enhancement algorithm is designed to enhance the details of human
body movements. Finally, a CNN-LSTM hybrid network architecture with a temporal-attention is constructed
for action classification. Experimental results show that the proposed micro-motion enhancement algorithm
improves recognition accuracy by 27.1% and 2.3%, compared to two traditional time–frequency analysis
methods. Furthermore, MPAR task in occlusion scenarios achieves recognition accuracy of 93.5%. In summary,
proposed framework not only retains the inherent advantages of millimeter-wave radar but also significantly
enhances multi-person activity recognition in complex scenarios.
1. Introduction
In recent years, human activity recognition (HAR) has become
crucial in applications like smart homes, elderly care, security, and
emergency rescue [1–4]. HAR methods typically rely on wearable
sensors or non-contact devices [5–8]. Wearable sensors face challenges
like user compliance, loss, and false alarms. In contrast, non-contact
devices represented by millimeter-wave radar have gained attention
for its all-weather capability and non-visual nature, making it suitable
for low-light or extreme conditions [9]. Millimeter-wave radar also
reduces privacy concerns, making it ideal for sensitive environments,
such as bedrooms and bathrooms, where elderly individuals are at
higher risk [10–12].
Currently, HAR methods based on millimeter-wave radar can be
broadly categorized into two main types. One category consists of
feature map-based algorithms, which achieve recognition by captur-
ing micro-Doppler signatures associated with human motion. These
features stem from the relative movement between limbs and the
torso and can be obtained using different radar waveforms. Distinct
human actions, such as walking, crawling, and boxing, produce unique
micro-Doppler signals, enabling the differentiation of human motion

to classify seven different activities [16]. Since then, feature-map-
based radar HAR methods become widely applied. One study employs
a long short-term memory (LSTM) network architecture to classify
six different human activities, achieving an average recognition ac-
curacy of 90% [17]. Subsequently, network architectures combining
convolutional neural networks (CNN) with LSTM also began to be ap-
plied [18–20]. With the continued advancement of technology, various

an accuracy of 93% in recognizing six types of human activities gets
achieved. Later, features such as time-angle maps and distance-angle
maps get introduced, further enhancing the recognition accuracy of
HAR [22–24]. However, feature-map-based algorithms for HAR typi-
cally use single-input single-output (SISO) radar data, which generally
lacks spatial attributes. As a result, these methods are mainly suited for
single-person activity recognition, as the features of different activities
tend to overlap in multi-person scenarios. This limitation restricts the
application of such algorithms in MPAR.
Additionally, in [25], point clouds are voxelized into uniform grids
and processed with a CNN-LSTM network, achieving 90.47% recogni-
tion accuracy for five action types. Furthermore, [26] optimizes the
classification model, improving real-time recognition accuracy. Kim
et al. [27] employ high-resolution radar-generated human activity
point clouds, feeding them into a DCNN network, and achieve a 97% ac-
curacy for seven actions. In MPAR domain, [28] focuses on recognizing
behaviors of multiple patients by extracting velocity information from
point clouds and converting it into time-Doppler maps, achieving high
accuracy. Further extending this approach, [29] incorporates additional
features—such as distance, horizontal angle, and pitch angle—into
time-Doppler, time-distance, time-horizontal angle, and time-pitch an-
gle maps. These feature maps are then input into a classification
network, achieving a multi-target recognition accuracy of 93.97%.
Finally, [30] tackles the challenge of separating point clouds from mul-
tiple targets and reduces them from 3D to 2D, achieving an accuracy
of 92.2% for classifying five types of actions. Although current MPAR
methods achieve significant progress in recognition accuracy, they
remain highly dependent on environmental robustness. While effective
in recognizing multi-person activities, these methods [28–30] do not
adequately account for potential issues such as occlusion and crossover
of moving targets. When such disturbances occur, the recognition
accuracy often decreases significantly. Furthermore, [28,29] only map
the feature information of the point cloud and generate feature maps,
leading to the insufficient capture of micro-motion features, which
further impacts the accuracy of recognition.
In summary, although prior MPAR methods have achieved promis-
ing recognition accuracy, they still face two major limitations: fea-
ture aliasing caused by overlapping targets in multi-person scenarios,
and the insufficient capture of fine-grained micro-motion information
from point clouds. To address these challenges, this paper proposes a
novel MPAR framework that combines point cloud separation, dynamic
micro-Doppler mapping, and temporal-attention-based classification.
The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:
• A spatiotemporal adaptive RDM binding (STAR-Bind) is proposed
to effectively separate multi-person motion features in complex
scenarios and significantly preserve the micro-movement charac-
teristics of each target.
• A torso micro-motion recognition enhancement algorithm (TM-
REA) is designed to improve the visibility of different types of
HAR feature maps.
• A network CNN_LSTM with temporal-attention (TA-CL) is specif-
ically constructed for millimeter-wave radar-based HAR, thereby
enhancing the recognition accuracy.
• This paper employs a frequency-modulated continuous wave
(FMCW) millimeter-wave radar to collect motion datasets from
different individuals across various scenarios, demonstrating the
algorithm’s robustness in multi-person action recognition within
complex environments.

2.1.1. Point clouds generation
After obtaining RDM, constant false alarm rate (CFAR) detection
is performed to obtain the target’s speed and range information. In
our work, data collection is performed using Texas Instruments (TI)
IWR6843ISK-ODS radar, which consists of four receiving antennas and
three transmitting antennas. Its virtual antenna layout can be used to
measure angles, as shown in Fig. 2. Then, an angle estimation algorithm
is applied to the target data from different virtual antennas to obtain
angle information. MUSIC [32] super-resolution angle estimation algo-
rithm is applied to overcome the Rayleigh limit of traditional FFT-based
methods.

2.1.2. Clustering
Density-based spatial clustering of applications with noise (DB-
SCAN) algorithm has become a cornerstone in radar point cloud pro-
cessing, particularly for its dual capability in cluster identification and
noise suppression [33]. Unlike centroid-based approaches that rely on
prior knowledge of cluster quantities, DBSCAN autonomously distin-
guishes targets from background noise through density connectivity
analysis, which is a key advantage in millimeter-wave radar applica-
tions where multipath reflections cause spatially heterogeneous noise
distributions.
However, the algorithm’s performance is highly sensitive to the
selection of two critical parameters: the neighborhood radius (𝜖) and
minimum cluster size (MinPts). To address this, we propose an adaptive
parameter optimization framework. In the first phase, the statistical
𝜖 estimation method determines 𝜖 as the 90th percentile of k-nearest
neighbor (k-NN) distances across the point cloud, ensuring an optimal
balance between target coverage and noise rejection. In the second
phase, the dynamic scaling of MinPts adapts the minimum cluster size to
5% of the total point count, allowing the algorithm to remain sensitive
to the complexity of the scene.
3. Methodology
The proposed MPAR framework clusters the point cloud to separate
different targets and uses Kalman filter (KF) in combination with
the joint probability data association (JPDA) algorithm [34] for data
association. It predicts the state of each target at the next time step
and associates the information accordingly. Then each target’s feature
information is mapped to the original RDM in each frame, features
from other targets are removed to obtain a clean RDM for each target.
Furthermore, these RDMs are projected onto the Doppler dimension to
create time-Doppler maps, which are processed using the TMREA torso
micro-motion enhancement algorithm proposed in this work, yielding
more effective micro-Doppler maps. Finally, these enhanced micro-
Doppler maps are fed into TA-CL network for activity classification, as
illustrated in Fig. 3.
3.1. Spatiotemporal adaptive RDM binding
The principal challenge in synthesizing temporal-Doppler sequences
stems from the dynamic association of target tracking states subse-
quent to JPDA-processed RDM data. To address this issue, a spa-
tiotemporal adaptive RDM binding (STAR-Bind) method is presented.
It enables discriminative target characterization through KF-driven re-
verse localization within the RDM coordinate system, augmented by
multidimensional constraint mechanisms. The proposed methodology,
STAR-Bind, is architected around three principal components: multi-
target tracking framework, RDM coordinate mapping, and multi-target
RDM separation.

3.3. Occlusion modeling and handling mechanism
To address radar echo degradation caused by mutual occlusion
among multiple moving targets, this framework introduces a multi-
level coordination mechanism to ensure temporal feature continuity.

to be predicted using the Kalman filter. Although monostatic radar can-
not receive echoes from fully occluded targets, the joint probabilistic
data association (JPDA) method retains a robust hypothesis through
the association probability 𝛽𝑗𝑘. A designated occlusion flag weight 𝛽0 is
introduced to reduce the influence of missing observations and preserve
trajectory continuity.

3.4. 2D-CNN_LSTM classification model with temporal-attention
To achieve high accuracy of recognition, we design a deep learning
model TA-CL, which is specifically intended to capture and model
multi-level spatio-temporal features in radar data, as illustrated in
Fig. 5.
The input feature maps are first processed through a 3-layer CNN
for multi-scale feature extraction. Each convolutional layer uses a 3 × 3
kernel, with the number of channels increasing progressively to expand
the receptive field. To enhance feature representation, a Leaky-ReLU
activation function is applied after each convolutional layer, introduc-
ing non-saturating nonlinearity to mitigate gradient sparsity issues.
Additionally, local response normalization is incorporated between
convolutional layers to improve feature contrast, while max pooling
layers (with a 2 × 2 kernel and a stride of 2) are employed for spatial
downsampling and translation invariance, ultimately producing robust
low-level feature representations.
For each action instance, we construct a time series of 𝑇 = 90
frames from the time–Doppler map (TDM) sequence. Each TDM frame
𝐈𝑡 is independently passed through the CNN backbone to obtain a
corresponding spatial feature vector 𝐅𝑡 ∈ R𝑑. The resulting sequence
{𝐅1, 𝐅2, … , 𝐅𝑇 } is stacked to form a feature matrix 𝐒 ∈ R𝑇 ×𝑑, which
serves as the input to the LSTM module. For longer recordings, we
adopt a sliding window strategy with a window length of 90 and a
stride of 10 frames to enhance data diversity and temporal alignment.
To address the strong temporal correlations in radar data, a stacked
LSTM network is designed as a temporal feature parser. The spatial
features output by the DCNN are serialized and fed into the LSTM
units, where the gating mechanisms (input gate, forget gate, output
gate) dynamically regulate the cell state (𝑐𝑡) and hidden state (ℎ𝑡),
explicitly modeling long- and short-term dependencies. By vertically
stacking multiple LSTM layers, the network can hierarchically abstract
high-order temporal patterns, capturing the progressive evolution of
target actions.
To overcome the limitation of traditional LSTMs in selectively fo-
cusing on key time steps, a hierarchical temporal attention mechanism
is proposed:
1. Feature Compression: Max pooling and average pooling are
applied along the time axis to the LSTM output feature matrix,
generating dual-channel temporal saliency descriptors.
2. Weight Generation: The dual-channel descriptors are fed into
a shared-weight MLP, and a Sigmoid function normalizes the
output to produce the temporal attention weight vector 𝛼𝑡.

4.2. Dataset establishment

a range of 1 to 3.5 meters from the radar, and their lateral distance
from millimeter-wave radar does not exceed 3 m, ensuring they remain
within the radar’s field of view throughout the activity.
To validate the generalizability and robustness of our method, we
recruit 20 test subjects with varying heights, weights, and genders,
including 14 males and 6 females. Detailed information is provided in
Table 2.
Initially, individual actions are collected from each test subject.
These construct the dataset Data-A, which aims to provide a diverse

each scenario. Eight common types of human activities are collected,

A4: Jogging, A5: Kicking, A6: Sitting, A7: Standing, and A8: Walking.

‘‘Crouching’’ (A2) versus ‘‘falling’’ (A3), and ‘‘boxing’’ (A1) versus

analysis reveals that this phenomenon is attributed to micro-motion
confusion between similar action categories. Specifically, ‘‘squatting’’

‘‘kicking’’ and ‘‘boxing’’ share highly similar upper-limb trajectories
and rhythmic patterns. This similarity results in ambiguity during
fine-grained action classification.
To address this issue, the proposed TA-CL model incorporates a
temporal attention mechanism that enables differentiated weighting of
temporal features. Specifically, this mechanism adaptively focuses on
discriminative key frames within action sequences (such as gait tran-
sitions, limb acceleration peaks, or the onset of a striking movement)
rather than uniformly processing all temporal information. These key
frames contain significant inter-class discriminative features, essential
for accurately distinguishing between visually similar actions.
Experimental validation demonstrates that this mechanism substan-
tially enhances inter-class differentiation. In particular, classification
accuracy significantly improves for challenging action pairs, such as
squatting versus falling (A2 vs. A3) and boxing versus kicking (A1 vs.
A5), by effectively highlighting discriminative temporal characteristics.
4.4.2. Comparison of temporal modeling approaches
To systematically validate the comprehensive advantages of the
TA-CL model in terms of temporal sequence modeling capability and
potential for lightweight deployment, we designed an ablation study
focusing on different temporal network variants. Specifically, we main-
tained the spatial feature extraction module (TA-CNN) as the baseline
architecture, and sequentially replaced the original LSTM temporal
units with gated recurrent units (GRU) [35], bidirectional gated recur-
rent units (Bi-GRU), Transformer encoders [36], and bidirectional long
short-term memory networks (Bi-LSTM). By rigorously comparing the
performance of these temporal modules given identical spatial feature
inputs, we quantitatively evaluated our model’s dual enhancement in
terms of capturing complex action sequence features (measured by clas-
sification accuracy) and model compactness (measured by Params/MB,
FLOPs).
As shown in Table 4, the proposed TA-CL model achieves the
highest recognition accuracy (97.2%) while maintaining a modest pa-
rameter count (370K) and relatively low computational complexity
(134M FLOPs). Although both Bi-LSTM and Transformer configurations
reach comparable accuracy (97.1%), they require significantly more
parameters and higher FLOPs. In contrast, GRU-based models are more
lightweight but do not achieve the same level of accuracy as LSTM-
based approaches. Overall, the LSTM architecture, when combined with
temporal attention, offers an effective balance between accuracy and
efficiency, making it the most suitable choice for micro-Doppler radar
sequence modeling in this work.

5. Conclusion
This paper presented an MPAR algorithm that integrates spatial
point cloud and micro-Doppler features. Through improved cluster-
ing, tracking, and data association techniques, the proposed method
achieves effective separation of multi-target point cloud features and
constructs time-Doppler feature maps using a dynamic projection mech-
anism. Additionally, a trunk-based micro-Doppler enhancement algo-
rithm was designed to significantly improve feature representation.
A hybrid CNN-LSTM network with integrated temporal attention was
further developed for robust activity classification.
Experimental results demonstrated that the proposed framework
significantly enhances feature separability and recognition accuracy,
particularly under occlusion scenarios, thereby addressing key lim-
itations of previous millimeter-wave radar-based MPAR approaches.
However, the current work is limited by the angular resolution of the
single-radar system, which constrains the scalability to larger numbers
of closely spaced targets. Furthermore, the classification network pri-
marily employs conventional deep learning backbones, which may not
fully exploit the rich spatiotemporal information in more challenging
scenarios.
In future work, we will focus on extending the system to support
a greater number of targets by employing higher-resolution or cas-
caded MIMO radar platforms. We also plan to investigate advanced
classification networks, such as transformer-based architectures and
self-supervised learning approaches, to further improve recognition
performance and generalization. These advancements are expected to
make the proposed MPAR framework more versatile and robust for
practical applications in dynamic, densely populated, or unconstrained
environments.
